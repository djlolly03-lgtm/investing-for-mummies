What the registration page is costing you

investingformummies.com/registernow — served as register-v2.html, audited from the Instagram link-in-bio URL, desktop + mobile, source and rendered DOM. Benchmarked against webinar.finkhoz.com and workshop.digitalscholar.in/agentic-ai-mastery.

Verdict

The page is a well-built catalogue, not a landing page. It answers “which batch?” cleanly and quickly. It never answers the question a woman arriving cold from Instagram is actually asking: who is teaching this, and why should I trust her with ₹19,000?

Three things need fixing today rather than in a redesign. One of them is quoting September buyers ₹19,000 and charging them ₹21,000.

Part one · your page

Critical

3 findings · fix today

These are live right now, on a page taking payments from a running Instagram campaign.

01

September buyers are shown ₹19,000 and charged ₹21,000

The hero shows one flat price for all four batches: ₹21,000 struck through → ₹19,000, “Early bird — till 20 Aug 2026.” But the checkout prices each batch separately, and the September early-bird deadline passed seven days ago. October is still discounted; September is not.

So a woman clicks “Reserve” on September B1 having just read ₹19,000, and the payment button says Pay & Register — ₹21,000. That is a ₹2,000 surprise at the exact moment of payment, from traffic that has no prior relationship with you.

register-v2.html hero → ₹19,000 · "Early bird — till 20 Aug 2026" (all 4 batches)
ifm form → SEP_EARLY_BIRD_DEADLINE = 2026-08-20 // expired
ifm form → OCT_EARLY_BIRD_DEADLINE = 2026-09-20 // live
checkout → "I agree to register and confirm payment of ₹21,000"
FixPrice the batch cards individually — September ₹21,000, October ₹19,000 with “early bird till 20 Sep”. Kill the blanket hero price. Either way the “till 20 Aug” date must come off the page today; it makes even the discount you are still honouring look stale.
02

Nothing is measuring this. The Instagram campaign is invisible

The URL you sent carries utm_source=ig, utm_medium=social, utm_content=link_in_bio and an fbclid. The page reads none of them, stores none of them, and passes none of them to the registration form. There is no Meta pixel, no Google Analytics, no GTM, no dataLayer — on either the landing page or the checkout page.

Concretely: you cannot tell how many people hit this page, how many reached checkout, how many paid, or that a single rupee came from Instagram. You are also building no retargeting audience — every warm visitor who doesn’t buy is lost permanently, which is normally the cheapest money in a funnel like this.

Both competitors do this properly, and Finkhoz’s implementation is worth copying outright → part five.

registernow → fbq 0 · gtag 0 · gtm 0 · dataLayer 0
/ifm form → fbq 0 · gtag 0 · utm_ 0 · fbclid 0
reserve link → /ifm?batch=B3 — utm params dropped at the click
FixMeta pixel + GA4 on both pages; forward the query string through the Reserve links; fire a Purchase / Lead event on the Razorpay success callback. This is a half-day of work and it is the difference between running a campaign and guessing at one.
03

You ask people to accept terms that don’t exist

The checkout has a consent checkbox and an error state reading “Please accept the terms to continue.” There are no terms. Neither the landing page nor the form links to a Terms & Conditions, Privacy Policy, or Refund/Cancellation policy — the footer of the landing page contains exactly one link, back to the homepage.

Your own FAQ compounds it: “we’ll work with you to either reschedule to a later batch or process a refund as per our cancellation policy” — a policy that is nowhere published. For a page collecting ₹21,000 through Razorpay, that’s both a trust problem and a gap against what payment gateways normally expect a merchant to display. Both competitors publish all three policies → steal 05.

policy links on registernow → none
policy links on /ifm form → none
footer contents → "www.investingformummies.com" (1 link)
FixPublish the three policies, link them in the footer and next to the consent checkbox, and add the legal entity + GSTIN (Mooga Tech Solutions India Pvt Ltd, 27AAECM9012F1ZM) and the Nariman Point address to the footer.
Part two · your page

Important

6 findings · this week

These are what the redesign is really for. Nothing here is broken code — it’s absent persuasion.

04

There is no proof of any kind on the page

No testimonials. No founder. No photographs of a cohort. No “X women trained”, no ratings, no alumni quotes, no credentials. Hiral Goel — the entire reason this course is worth ₹21,000 rather than a free YouTube playlist — is not mentioned once.

This is the single biggest gap. You are asking a stranger from Instagram for ₹21,000 and eight weekday mornings, and the page’s only argument is a syllabus. Both competitors put their teacher above the fold → steal 02.

FixA founder block with a real photo and why-she-teaches-this; three named testimonials from past cohorts; one honest number you can actually stand behind. You have the workshop photos and videos already — they’re sitting in the repo unused.
05

The page is entirely text — two images, both logos

For a brand whose line is “nurturing financially confident women”, the registration page is a spreadsheet. No venue photo, no classroom, no printed handbook, no faces. Warmth is the brand’s whole differentiator against every bank and finfluencer, and none of it is on the page where money changes hands.

document.images → 2 (round logo + wordmark, both in the header)
<video> → 0   page height → 7,749px of near-pure text
FixShow the room, the handbook, and the women. Even three good photographs would change this page’s temperature completely.
06

Shared on WhatsApp, this link renders as a bare URL

Treat this one as critical. It is filed under Important because that’s its usual severity — but IFM does not grow the usual way. Students arrive through Hiral’s network, friends of friends, and word of mouth; Instagram is where they verify you afterwards, not where they find you. Word of mouth in this market travels on WhatsApp, which makes a forwarded link your actual acquisition channel.

And it degrades at exactly the handoff. No Open Graph tags, no Twitter card, no share image — so when one woman sends this to a friend, it arrives as a naked URL with no title, no image, no description. The referral survives; the persuasion doesn’t.

og: tags 0  ·  twitter: tags 0  ·  canonical 0
Fixog:title, og:description, og:image (1200×630 with the batch dates baked in), plus a canonical URL so the utm/fbclid variants don’t fragment.
07

The page has no h1

A 7,749-pixel commercial page carries a single h2 and zero h1. “Choose your programme” is styled text in a div. That costs you on search, and screen-reader users get no document outline to navigate by. It’s also a wasted position — the h1 is where the competitors put their sharpest line → steal 01.

h1 0  ·  h2 1  ·  h3 0  ·  ld+json schema 0
FixReal heading hierarchy, plus Course and FAQPage structured data — you already have the FAQ content written, so the rich result is nearly free.
08

September B1 starts in five days and the page is completely calm about it

The first session is 1 September. Nothing on the page conveys that. Your FAQ says batches are capped at 10–12 people — genuine, credible scarcity — but the batch cards never show seats remaining, and the only urgency device on the page is an early-bird deadline that already expired → steal 07.

FixLive seat counts on the batch cards (the checkout already tracks seats — loadSeats('batch4')), a “starts in 5 days” marker, and honest urgency instead of an expired one.
09

“Reserve →” on three programmes you can’t actually reserve

Introductory, Young Adults and Teens all show “Next batch — TBA” and a Reserve → button. The button actually leads to a waitlist/notify form. The word promises a booking and delivers a mailing list — a small broken promise, right where you're asking for trust.

/iya live-batches-meta → chips: [] — nothing bookable
/ift live-batches-meta → chips: [] — nothing bookable
/iya page contains → "SOLD OUT" · "Waitlist" · "Notify"
FixLabel the button by what it does: “Notify me →”. And use these three as email capture — they’re the only lead-generation surface on an otherwise pay-or-leave page.
Part three · your page

Polish

7 findings · fold into the redesign
10

A visible copy bug: “Next batch — Next batch dates TBA”

The live-sync script prefixes “Next batch — ” onto a chip whose text is already “Next batch dates TBA”. It renders on the Introductory card right now.

FixStrip the prefix when the chip already contains it, or return a bare date string from the meta.
11

The page has drifted off the brand system

It’s set in Poppins, not the brand’s Nunito + Lora. The teal is #5ec4ba, not the brand’s #2a9d8f. And there are two different navies inside this one file — the page variables use #1a4a5a while the site header nav uses the correct #1a3a5c.

font-family → Poppins (brand: Nunito + Lora)
--ifm-teal → #5ec4ba (brand: #2a9d8f)
--ifm-navy → #1a4a5a vs .sh-nav → #1a3a5c
FixReconcile to the tokens in IFM Brand Guidelines 2026 §3 — that reconciliation was already done once across the games codebase.
12

Every visit downloads three extra pages to update three words

On load, the page fetches /register/ifm-intro, /iya and /ift in full — three extra round trips, roughly 52 KB compressed against the page’s own 8 KB — purely to refresh the three “next batch” pills, which all currently resolve to “TBA” anyway.

page itself → 8,042 B   ttfb 0.66s
sync fetches → 10,717 + 20,212 + 21,434 = 52,363 B
FixServe one small JSON endpoint with all batch states, or bake the state at deploy time. The architecture is sound; the payload is not.
13

Mobile is four tall, near-identical cards and no persistent CTA

At 375px the batch cards stack full-width and very tall, so the four of them are a long, repetitive scroll before anything else — curriculum, FAQ, trust — is reached. There is no sticky “Reserve” bar, so a user deep in the FAQ has to scroll all the way back up to act.

FixCompress the cards to a scannable row each, and add a sticky bottom CTA once the user is past the hero.
14

The two holiday skips are correct but unlabelled

September B2 runs Mon & Wed but skips Mon 14 Sep. October B1 runs Tue & Thu but skips Tue 20 Oct. Both are deliberate holiday skips — confirmed. The dates are right; the page just doesn’t say why they break pattern.

That matters more than it sounds. Your buyer is scanning these dates against school runs and family commitments, and an irregular list with no explanation reads as carelessness — on a page that already has an expired discount on it → finding 01. Labelling the gap turns an apparent mistake into evidence that you plan around her calendar.

Sep B2 → 2, 7, 9, ·14 holiday· 16, 21, 23, 28, 30
Oct B1 → 1, 6, 8, 13, 15, ·20 holiday· 22, 27, 29
FixName the holiday inline on the batch card — “no session, 14 Sep (holiday)”. One line each, and it removes the doubt instead of leaving her to wonder.
15

Dead August data still in the checkout

BATCH_LABELS still contains the finished August B1 and B2 batches, and a code comment still maps “B1 → batch2 (August B1)”. Not user-visible today — those cards no longer render — but it’s exactly the kind of stale mapping that produces a wrong-batch booking next time someone edits this file.

FixRemove past batches when they close, as part of the batch rollover routine.
16

The homepage this page links to is still the rolled-back build

Outside the scope of this page, but it’s one click away via the logo: investingformummies.com/ is still serving the old pre-rebuild snapshot — 255,614 bytes, unchanged since 11 August. That’s the regression your checklist has been tracking for seventeen days, and it still re-breaks seven previously-fixed items including the duplicated footer legal bar and the black video thumbnails.

FixRedeploy the 8 August build. One action, fixes all seven.

Part six · the competitors

Steal these

7 · ranked by impact

Ordered by how much each is likely to move registrations, not by effort.

01
Finkhoz · headline

Open with her problem, not your catalogue

Finkhoz leads with a question the reader is already asking herself. Your page leads with an instruction to the reader about how to use your website.

Confused between SIPs, Gold and Mutual Funds?Finkhoz h1

You already own a better version of this line — “You don’t need to know everything to begin” — and it’s currently set in 13px italic grey, above the fold, and then abandoned. That sentence is your headline. It answers the real objection, which isn’t “what’s the syllabus” but “I’ll feel stupid.”

DoPromote the tagline to a real h1 → finding 07. Follow it with the outcome: what she can do on day 9 that she can’t do today.
02
Both pages

Put Hiral on the page

Finkhoz names Aarzoo above the fold and gives her a full bio block with five credential lines and a positioning sentence — “she teaches the way a smart friend would.” Digital Scholar does the same at greater length. Both understand that in education, the person is the product.

Your page doesn’t mention Hiral Goel once. For a ₹21,000 in-person course, that is the single biggest hole → finding 04 — bigger than the pricing bug, though slower to hurt you.

DoPhoto, three sentences on why she teaches this, and the credentials. Then say plainly whether IFM is SEBI-registered or explicitly education-only — Finkhoz leads with that badge because in this category it settles the trust question fast.
03
Finkhoz · “Sound familiar?”

Name the problem in her words before you sell the fix

Four bullets, each a sentence a real person would say to herself: salary comes in, vanishes by month-end, and you can’t say where. Then the bridge — “You don’t need a finance degree to fix this.” It costs one screen and it earns the right to everything after it.

Your equivalents are easy and you know them better than Finkhoz does: the household where she isn’t told what the investments are; the SIP someone sold her that she can’t explain; the sense that this conversation happens over her head.

DoAdd one “Sound familiar?” block between the hero and the batch cards. Write it in her voice, not yours.
04
Both pages

Attach an outcome to every session

Your curriculum is already the strongest thing on your page — eight sessions, specific and concrete. Both competitors do one thing you don’t: they promise a result per unit, not a topic per unit.

By the end of this module, you will use Claude like an operator, not a consumer.Digital Scholar · every module

Digital Scholar also ends each module with a “★ Build:” line — a tangible thing you walk away holding. Finkhoz frames the whole curriculum as “every block ends with something you can actually use on your next salary credit.”

DoOne outcome line per session. Session 2 isn’t “insurance made simple” — it’s “you’ll know whether your existing policies are protection or a bad investment, and what to do about it.”
05
Both pages · footer

Publish the legal layer — both of them did

Finkhoz publishes a Refund Policy, Privacy Policy, Terms, and an unusually thorough risk disclaimer written against the DPDP Act 2023 and the Consumer Protection Act. Digital Scholar carries Privacy, Terms and Refund Policy plus a trademark disclaimer.

You publish none, while your checkout says “Please accept the terms to continue” and your FAQ cites a cancellation policy that doesn’t exist → finding 03. Two out of two competitors treat this as table stakes.

DoUse Finkhoz’s as the scope template — it’s written for exactly your regulatory context.
06
Finkhoz · offer block

Itemise what ₹21,000 actually buys

Finkhoz lists six components with individual prices totalling ₹6,395, then prices the lot at ₹99. The arithmetic is theatre, but the underlying move is sound: an unbundled offer feels bigger than a single number.

Your ₹21,000 currently buys “8 days · 2 hrs” — which reads as a time cost, not a value. It actually includes a printed handbook, small-group teaching capped at 10–12, session notes and slides after every session, interactive games, WhatsApp support, and a venue in Nariman Point. None of that is itemised.

DoList the components. Skip the invented rupee values — you don’t need them and at your price point they’d cheapen it.
07
Finkhoz · closing CTA

Honest scarcity, stated once

Only 12 seats left in this cohort · Closes when fullFinkhoz · final CTA

You have the more credible version of this and don’t use it: your batches are genuinely capped at 10–12, your checkout already tracks seats, and September B1 starts in five days → finding 08. Right now the only urgency device on your page is an early-bird deadline that expired a week ago — which does the opposite of urgency. It signals nobody is minding the page.

DoSurface the real seat count on each batch card, and a “starts in 5 days” marker. Real scarcity, stated once, with no countdown-timer theatrics.
Part seven · the competitors

Don’t steal these

4 · wrong for your category

Digital Scholar sells AI skills. You sell financial education in India, which is a regulated conversation — several of its best-performing devices would be liabilities on your page.

!
Digital Scholar

Income-claim testimonials

“I earned ₹1 lakh in a single month.” “I’m proud to say that I earned ₹80,000.” These work for a course about billable skills. On a page teaching people to invest, testimonials about returns edge toward performance claims — the thing financial educators get into trouble for.

Notice how carefully Finkhoz avoids it: every testimonial is about clarity and behaviour change — “we finally feel like a team about money”, “I opened my first SIP the same week.” Not one mentions a return.

InsteadCollect testimonials about confidence, clarity, and the first action taken. They’re also more persuasive to your actual buyer.
!
Digital Scholar

Manufactured scarcity and price-rise threats

“Goes up the moment we cross 500. No second chances.” “You either move now, or you pay 7x later.” This is a high-pressure register that will read as predatory to a 40-year-old woman deciding whether to spend ₹21,000 of household money — the precise audience whose trust you’ve spent years building.

InsteadYour scarcity is real (10–12 seats). Real scarcity needs no adjectives.
!
Digital Scholar

The contempt-for-the-reader voice

“Most people will close this page in 12 seconds. Good. This was never written for them.” “Read this if you are tired of being average.” It’s an effective filter for an ambitious-founder audience. It is the exact opposite of “nurturing financially confident women,” and it would repel yours.

InsteadFinkhoz’s register is your register: “no jargon, no hype, just clarity” — a smart friend, not a drill sergeant.
!
Digital Scholar

The 6.4 MB page

Their landing page is 6.4 MB of HTML — roughly 800× yours. On the mid-range Android phones your audience is actually browsing from, that’s a slow, janky first impression. Your 8 KB / 0.66s page is one of the genuinely good things you have. Add photographs and proof, but don’t inherit a page builder’s bloat along with its layout.

InsteadKeep the hand-built page. Compress images properly and lazy-load below the fold.
Part eight

The advantage you’re not using

Nobody else in this comparison has this

Digital Scholar built a bespoke interactive widget — click the tools you use, watch it calculate the hours you’re losing — purely to create one moment of personalised, self-inflicted pain before the pitch. It’s the most sophisticated thing on their page.

You have twenty-odd interactive calculators and games already built — inflation, three buckets, SIP, retirement, goal planning — and not one of them appears on the page that sells the course. They sit on a separate subdomain, entirely outside the funnel.

This is the highest-leverage thing in this document. One embedded calculator — “what did last year cost your savings account?” — does three jobs at once that no amount of copywriting can: it creates the pain, it proves you can teach, and it demonstrates the classroom experience rather than describing it. It also gives you the free front-end rung your price ladder is missing, and something worth an email address.

The redesign brief

what to add that isn’t there at all

Ordered by how much each is likely to move registrations, not by effort.

The founder
Who is Hiral, and why her? A photo, a short story, the credential. This is the missing centre of the page.
Proof
Named testimonials from past cohorts, cohort photographs, and one honest number you can defend. No invented statistics — the earlier website prototypes had fabricated figures; don’t inherit them.
The outcome
The page sells a syllabus. It should sell a transformation: what will she be able to do on day 9 that she can’t do today?
The real objection
Not “what’s the syllabus” but “I’m bad with money and I’ll feel stupid.” Your tagline already answers it — “You don’t need to know everything to begin” — but it’s set in 13px italic grey above the fold and then never developed.
Scarcity
Seats remaining per batch. You cap at 10–12 and that is a genuinely good reason to book now.
One clear CTA
The hero currently offers two soft scroll links. Give it a single primary action, and a sticky one on mobile.
A trust bar
Policies, entity, GSTIN, address, WhatsApp. Standard for a page taking ₹21,000, and currently absent.
Measurement
Pixel, analytics, UTM pass-through, purchase events. Without it you cannot tell whether the redesign worked.
