What those two pages do that yours doesn’t

Both are paid-Instagram funnels, both audited at source. Finkhoz is the one that matters — same market, same beginner audience, same Razorpay stack, and a structure you can copy almost line for line. Digital Scholar is worth studying for two mechanics and worth ignoring for four.

The finding underneath all the others

Neither of these pages tries to close a big sale from a cold Reels click. Finkhoz asks for ₹99. Digital Scholar asks for ₹1,997. You ask for ₹21,000. Every design difference below is downstream of that one decision — they get to be persuasive over weeks, you have to do it in a single scroll.

Steal these

7 · ranked by impact

Ordered by how much each is likely to move registrations, not by effort.

01
Finkhoz · headline

Open with her problem, not your catalogue

Finkhoz leads with a question the reader is already asking herself. Your page leads with an instruction to the reader about how to use your website.

Confused between SIPs, Gold and Mutual Funds?Finkhoz h1

You already own a better version of this line — “You don’t need to know everything to begin” — and it’s currently set in 13px italic grey, above the fold, and then abandoned. That sentence is your headline. It answers the real objection, which isn’t “what’s the syllabus” but “I’ll feel stupid.”

DoPromote the tagline to a real h1. Follow it with the outcome: what she can do on day 9 that she can’t do today.
02
Both pages

Put Hiral on the page

Finkhoz names Aarzoo above the fold and gives her a full bio block with five credential lines and a positioning sentence — “she teaches the way a smart friend would.” Digital Scholar does the same at greater length. Both understand that in education, the person is the product.

Your page doesn’t mention Hiral Goel once. For a ₹21,000 in-person course, that is the single biggest hole — bigger than the pricing bug, though slower to hurt you.

DoPhoto, three sentences on why she teaches this, and the credentials. Then say plainly whether IFM is SEBI-registered or explicitly education-only — Finkhoz leads with that badge because in this category it settles the trust question fast.
03
Finkhoz · “Sound familiar?”

Name the problem in her words before you sell the fix

Four bullets, each a sentence a real person would say to herself: salary comes in, vanishes by month-end, and you can’t say where. Then the bridge — “You don’t need a finance degree to fix this.” It costs one screen and it earns the right to everything after it.

Your equivalents are easy and you know them better than Finkhoz does: the household where she isn’t told what the investments are; the SIP someone sold her that she can’t explain; the sense that this conversation happens over her head.

DoAdd one “Sound familiar?” block between the hero and the batch cards. Write it in her voice, not yours.
04
Both pages

Attach an outcome to every session

Your curriculum is already the strongest thing on your page — eight sessions, specific and concrete. Both competitors do one thing you don’t: they promise a result per unit, not a topic per unit.

By the end of this module, you will use Claude like an operator, not a consumer.Digital Scholar · every module

Digital Scholar also ends each module with a “★ Build:” line — a tangible thing you walk away holding. Finkhoz frames the whole curriculum as “every block ends with something you can actually use on your next salary credit.”

DoOne outcome line per session. Session 2 isn’t “insurance made simple” — it’s “you’ll know whether your existing policies are protection or a bad investment, and what to do about it.”
05
Both pages · footer

Publish the legal layer — both of them did

Finkhoz publishes a Refund Policy, Privacy Policy, Terms, and an unusually thorough risk disclaimer written against the DPDP Act 2023 and the Consumer Protection Act. Digital Scholar carries Privacy, Terms and Refund Policy plus a trademark disclaimer.

You publish none, while your checkout says “Please accept the terms to continue” and your FAQ cites a cancellation policy that doesn’t exist. Two out of two competitors treat this as table stakes.

DoThis is already item 3 on your critical list. These two pages are useful templates for scope — particularly Finkhoz’s, which is written for exactly your regulatory context.
06
Finkhoz · offer block

Itemise what ₹21,000 actually buys

Finkhoz lists six components with individual prices totalling ₹6,395, then prices the lot at ₹99. The arithmetic is theatre, but the underlying move is sound: an unbundled offer feels bigger than a single number.

Your ₹21,000 currently buys “8 days · 2 hrs” — which reads as a time cost, not a value. It actually includes a printed handbook, small-group teaching capped at 10–12, session notes and slides after every session, interactive games, WhatsApp support, and a venue in Nariman Point. None of that is itemised.

DoList the components. Skip the invented rupee values — you don’t need them and at your price point they’d cheapen it.
07
Finkhoz · closing CTA

Honest scarcity, stated once

Only 12 seats left in this cohort · Closes when fullFinkhoz · final CTA

You have the more credible version of this and don’t use it: your batches are genuinely capped at 10–12, your checkout already tracks seats, and September B1 starts in five days. Right now the only urgency device on your page is an early-bird deadline that expired a week ago — which does the opposite of urgency. It signals nobody is minding the page.

DoSurface the real seat count on each batch card, and a “starts in 5 days” marker. Real scarcity, stated once, with no countdown-timer theatrics.

Don’t steal these

4 · wrong for your category

Digital Scholar sells AI skills. You sell financial education in India, which is a regulated conversation — several of its best-performing devices would be liabilities on your page.

!
Digital Scholar

Income-claim testimonials

“I earned ₹1 lakh in a single month.” “I’m proud to say that I earned ₹80,000.” These work for a course about billable skills. On a page teaching people to invest, testimonials about returns edge toward performance claims — the thing financial educators get into trouble for.

Notice how carefully Finkhoz avoids it: every testimonial is about clarity and behaviour change — “we finally feel like a team about money”, “I opened my first SIP the same week.” Not one mentions a return.

InsteadCollect testimonials about confidence, clarity, and the first action taken. They’re also more persuasive to your actual buyer.
!
Digital Scholar

Manufactured scarcity and price-rise threats

“Goes up the moment we cross 500. No second chances.” “You either move now, or you pay 7x later.” This is a high-pressure register that will read as predatory to a 40-year-old woman deciding whether to spend ₹21,000 of household money — the precise audience whose trust you’ve spent years building.

InsteadYour scarcity is real (10–12 seats). Real scarcity needs no adjectives.
!
Digital Scholar

The contempt-for-the-reader voice

“Most people will close this page in 12 seconds. Good. This was never written for them.” “Read this if you are tired of being average.” It’s an effective filter for an ambitious-founder audience. It is the exact opposite of “nurturing financially confident women,” and it would repel yours.

InsteadFinkhoz’s register is your register: “no jargon, no hype, just clarity” — a smart friend, not a drill sergeant.
!
Digital Scholar

The 6.4 MB page

Their landing page is 6.4 MB of HTML — roughly 800× yours. On the mid-range Android phones your audience is actually browsing from, that’s a slow, janky first impression. Your 8 KB / 0.66s page is one of the genuinely good things you have. Add photographs and proof, but don’t inherit a page builder’s bloat along with its layout.

InsteadKeep the hand-built page. Compress images properly and lazy-load below the fold.

The advantage you’re not using

Nobody else in this comparison has this

Digital Scholar built a bespoke interactive widget — click the tools you use, watch it calculate the hours you’re losing — purely to create one moment of personalised, self-inflicted pain before the pitch. It’s the most sophisticated thing on their page.

You have twenty-odd interactive calculators and games already built — inflation, three buckets, SIP, retirement, goal planning — and not one of them appears on the page that sells the course. They sit on a separate subdomain, entirely outside the funnel.

This is the highest-leverage thing on either list. One embedded calculator — “what did last year cost your savings account?” — does three jobs at once that no amount of copywriting can: it creates the pain, it proves you can teach, and it demonstrates the classroom experience rather than describing it. It also gives you the free front-end rung your price ladder is missing, and something worth an email address.
