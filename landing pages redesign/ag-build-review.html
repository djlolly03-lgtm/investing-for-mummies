Verdict

This is the rebuild spec executed well. The positioning, the headline, the founder block, the pain section, the outcome lines, the working calculator, the itemised offer, per-batch pricing, the labelled holiday skips — nearly all of it is there, and the writing is good. The persuasion layer is essentially done.

But it is a prototype, not a shippable page. Every link on it is an in-page anchor — there is no way to book, no way to reach the call, no policy links. Four blocks still contain visible placeholder text. The classroom gallery is broken. And several proof elements need checking before they go in front of anyone.

Blockers

4 · cannot publish as-is
01

Nothing on the page links anywhere

Every single href in the document is an in-page anchor. There is no booking link, no Calendly link, no WhatsApp, Instagram or email link, and the Refund / Privacy / Terms items in the footer are plain text, not links.

So the two CTAs the whole page is built around — Reserve a seat and Pick a time with Hiral — both just scroll you further down the same page. In its current state the page cannot take a booking or a call.

hrefs found → #batches · #call · #curriculum · #familiar · #faq · #hiral · #includes · #room · #top · #voices
external → fonts.googleapis.com · fonts.gstatic.com · claude.com/product/design
booking / calendly / wa.me / mailto → none
<form> → 0   <input> → 1 (the calculator)
FixPoint each batch card at /ifm?batch=B3…B6, the call CTA at the real Calendly URL, and wire the footer links. Carry the query string through so referral sources survive the click.
02

Placeholder notes are visible to visitors

Two internal to-dos are rendered as body copy, not comments — a reader sees them:

TBC: state SEBI status here in one line — registered, or education-only. Decide before publishing.In the “what we are, plainly” block
Investing for Mummies is a financial education programme and provides no investment advice. [SEBI status line — to confirm]Footer

This is the open decision from the audit surfacing in the page itself. It needs answering, not displaying.

FixDecide the SEBI wording, write the line, delete both placeholders.
03

Two of the four batch cards are placeholders — and the real data exists

September B2 shows “Seats to confirm”, “Days & time to confirm”, “Eight dates across September — to confirm” and “Holiday skips to be named inline”. October B2 shows “Eight dates across October — to confirm”.

That’s a regression against your current live page, which already publishes September B2 correctly: Mon & Wed, 11:00 AM – 1:00 PM, Sep 2, 7, 9, 16, 21, 23, 28, 30. Half the batches on a booking page reading “to confirm” four days before the cohort starts reads as an abandoned page.

FixFill both from the live checkout data. Nothing needs deciding — it’s already published elsewhere.
04

The classroom gallery is broken — all 12 images fail

“The room — this is what it actually looks like” builds its two drifting rows from twelve Google Drive images hotlinked at lh3.googleusercontent.com/d/<id>=w900. They are not bundled with the page, and they return 403 in the published artifact — the console shows the failures.

So the section that proves the room exists renders empty. And even on a normal host this approach is fragile: Drive hotlinks depend on those files staying publicly shared and are rate-limited.

gallery images → 12, all from lh3.googleusercontent.com
console → Failed to load resource: 403
bundled images → 5 jpg + 1 png + 1 mp4 (the gallery is not among them)
FixHost the twelve photos with the site and reference them by relative path. You already have the originals in CLAUDE/proposals/assets/ and highlight-reel-assets/.

Verify before this goes public

5 · proof and claims

None of these are style notes. Each is a factual claim the page makes to a stranger being asked for ₹21,000.

05

A teen workshop photo is captioned as a flagship alumna

The image with alt text “Alumna — the wins”, used as a testimonial thumbnail under the heading “116 alumni · Filmed in the room on the last session”, is a photograph of a teenage classroom — a mixed-gender group of school-age students, shot from behind, at what is plainly an IFT or IYA session, not the mums flagship.

Two problems. It misrepresents who is in the room on a page selling the flagship. And these are minors — publishing children’s faces needs verifiable parental consent under the DPDP Act, which is a live concern for a brand selling trust to parents.

It’s also, on your own numbers, weak content: your worst-performing post ever was faceless teen-workshop footage shot from behind.

FixReplace with a flagship-cohort photo. The group shot already bundled with the page (alt “Alumna from the cohort”) is exactly right — real adult women, real room.
06

The three video quotes are the spec’s examples, presented as real footage

The page shows three video testimonials — “I finally read my own policy documents.” · “I opened my first SIP the same week.” · “I stopped nodding along.” — under the line “Filmed in the room on the last session.”

Those are verbatim the illustrative examples from the rebuild spec, written to show the kind of quote to collect. And the filming task is still outstanding — it’s scheduled for the last session of the September batch, which starts on the 1st. They cannot yet be real footage.

Presenting invented quotes as filmed testimony is the one thing I’d least want this brand to ship. It is also the exact failure mode flagged in the earlier prototypes.

FixPull the video block until the September filming happens, or mark it plainly as a layout placeholder. Do not publish it as-is.
07

Confirm who the “aha moment” alumna actually is

The third testimonial thumbnail (alt “Alumna — the aha moment”) is a posed, produced photograph of a woman seated with the printed IFM handbook — a brand shot, not candid classroom footage. Whoever she is, she is being presented as a course graduate giving testimony.

FixConfirm she is a real flagship alumna and has consented to appear as one. If she is staff, or the photo is a brand shoot, it cannot sit in the testimonial rail.
08

Seat counts are stated as fact

“4 of 12 seats left” on September B1 and “9 of 12 seats left” on October B1. This is exactly the honest scarcity the page should carry — but only if the numbers come from the checkout’s real seat data rather than being written in.

FixWire them to the live count (loadSeats('batch4') already holds it) or remove them. A scarcity number that stays frozen for three weeks is worse than none.
09

The four named testimonials need sign-off

Neha M. (Bandra), Celia (Juhu), Amisha Gandhi, Sakshi Jiwrajka. The quotes read as genuine and are correctly framed — every one is about clarity and confidence, none mentions returns. That discipline is right.

They still need confirming as real, with permission to publish name and location. Worth a specific check that Sakshi Jiwrajka is an alumna and not the team member of the same first name — a staff testimonial presented as a customer one would be costly if spotted.

13

It is now a very heavy page

Roughly 7 MB of bundled assets: 19 separate woff2 font files, and two founder photographs shipped at 1.4 MB and 1.1 MB uncompressed. Your current live page is 8 KB and serves in 0.66s.

Some of that is the artifact bundler inlining everything, and will shrink on a real host. The uncompressed photos and the 19 font files will not shrink on their own. On the mid-range Android phones your audience actually browses from, this is the bloat I flagged as the one thing not to copy from Digital Scholar.

fonts → 19 × woff2   images → 5 jpg + 1 png   video → 1 mp4 (2.9 MB)
largest → 1,429 KB and 1,103 KB — both “Hiral Goel”
FixSubset the fonts to Latin, compress the photos to ~200 KB, and lazy-load the video behind a poster frame.
14

The calculator shows one number before JavaScript, another after

The served HTML has “– ₹12,500” baked in as the headline figure for the ₹5,00,000 default. The script computes the loss as value × 5% and renders ₹25,000. So the number visibly jumps on load — and if the script fails, the wrong figure is what stays on screen.

₹12,500 is the shortfall after interest (5% inflation less 2.5% interest), which is what the caption underneath describes. The two numbers are both defensible; they’re just inconsistent with each other.

FixPick one figure as the headline and make the static HTML match what the script renders.
