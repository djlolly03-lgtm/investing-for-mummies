## 1 · HOOK — 0:00–0:07 · HER, full frame

> "Have you ever booked gold at today's rate for a wedding that's six months away?
> Then you already understand a futures contract. You just never called it that."

## 2 · THE ANALOGY — 0:07–0:24 · CARD `cJEWELLER`

> "You're at the jeweller. Gold is one lakh rupees today. The wedding is in May.
> You don't want to walk in in May and find it's one lakh twenty.
> So the two of you agree — this price, that date. He's locked in. You're locked in.
>
> That's it. A price agreed today, for something you'll take delivery of later.
> That is a futures contract."

**Layers, arriving on the word:**
- L0 on "one lakh rupees today" — TODAY · ₹1,00,000 · 10g
- L1 on "that date" — a date chip: MAY · locked
- L2 on "futures contract" — the two chips joined, label **A PRICE AGREED TODAY. GOODS LATER.**

## 3 · WHO USES IT, AND WHY — 0:24–0:38 · HER + panel `pTWO`

> "Two completely different people use the same contract.
> The first is you. The family planning a wedding. You're not trying to make money —
> you're trying not to get a shock. That's called hedging.
> The second has no wedding at all. He just thinks gold will go up and wants to profit
> from the move. That's speculating. Same contract. Opposite intention."

**Panel:** two columns — YOU: *avoiding a shock* / HIM: *chasing the move*.

## 4 · WHERE THE JEWELLER ANALOGY BREAKS — 0:38–1:00 · CARD `cBREAK`

> "But the stock-market version is not your jeweller. Three differences, and they're
> the whole story.
>
> One — you don't pay the full amount. You put down a deposit. That's called margin.
> A small amount of money now controls a very large position.
>
> Two — the exchange re-prices your contract every single day. If it moves against you,
> you top up that same evening. Not in May. Today.
>
> Three — almost nobody takes the gold. They close out before the date and settle the
> difference in cash."

**Layers:** ① MARGIN — *a deposit, not the full price* ② DAILY SETTLEMENT — *top up tonight*
③ NO DELIVERY — *settled in cash*. The third layer repaints to the summary line
**SMALL MONEY. BIG POSITION. DAILY CONSEQUENCES.** (hard cut, not a fade — §11d)

## 5 · THE HONEST NUMBER — 1:00–1:14 · CARD `cSEBI`

> "And that's the catch. When a deposit controls a big position, your gains multiply —
> and so do your losses.
>
> SEBI counted it. Last financial year, nearly nine out of ten individual traders in
> equity derivatives lost money. The average loss was over one lakh rupees.
> Most of that damage came from options, not futures — but it's the same door."

**Card:** one dominant number — **~88%** *lost money* · sub: average loss ₹1.17 lakh ·
footnote: SEBI, individual traders, equity derivatives, FY25-26

## 6 · CLOSE — 1:14–1:26 · HER, one unbroken shot, no card (§11a)

> "Futures were built for the farmer who wants a fixed price for his crop, and the
> business that needs to plan. They were not built to make you rich by Friday.
>
> You don't have to trade futures. You do need to know what one is when someone
> offers you one.
>
> I'm Hiral Goel, founder of Investing for Mummies. Follow along to understand money

## Why this analogy and not the others

- **Gold rate-booking** — the audience has done it or watched it being done, the stakes are
  real and emotional, and it is mechanically a forward contract. Honest, not approximate.
- Rejected: *mango pre-booking* (too small to carry the risk beat), *wedding hall advance*
  (price volatility is not felt), *insurance* (already used, and it is a different product).

## One weakness, stated plainly

"What is a futures contract" is an **evergreen explainer**, and the channel's own grid says
those sit at 500–1,500 views while news-led reels with a name in the headline do ~8×.
To give it a peg, open on the news instead of the question:

> "SEBI just published the number. Nine out of ten people trading derivatives lost money
> last year. Here's what they were actually trading — and why your jeweller already
> taught you how it works."

Same script from scene 2 onward.
