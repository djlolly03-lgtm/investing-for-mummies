# Games benchmark: per-example notes

Written 27 Oct 2026 for IFM's short browser games (2–10 min, solo, phone-first, traffic from Instagram and WhatsApp).
The ranked playbook is in `games-benchmark.json`. This file holds the evidence behind it.

**How to read the labels**
- **[played]**: I opened it and played or clicked through it in a browser on 27 Oct 2026.
- **[secondhand]**: built from press coverage, app-store listings or the maker's own blog. Treat the details as reported, not verified.
- Where a source was weak or missing, that is stated.

Each entry covers the same eight things: core loop · teaching without lecturing · juice · payoff · replay · share · mobile · why it would or would not travel on WhatsApp.

## A. Money and finance games

### 1. SPENT (playspent.org): McKinney for Urban Ministries of Durham, 2011 [played]
- **Core loop:** You start with $1,000 and must reach day 30. Each screen is one dilemma with two or three choices. Choices so far: pick a job from three swipeable cards (server $2.13/hr plus tips, warehouse $14/hr, office temp $15/hr), then a health plan (Gold, Silver or Bronze).
- **Teaching without lecturing:** After each choice a large circle titled "Result" gives two sentences of consequence with a real number (for example, the cheapest plan still costs about $76 a paycheck). The pay screen breaks down gross pay, tax, take-home and weekly pay before you have been taught any of those terms.
- **Juice:** Restrained. Big Balance number top-left, Day counter top-right, a 30-day calendar strip with paydays marked "$", three "job strike" crosses, and a sound toggle. Full-screen fact cards fade in between scenes ("Over 26 million Americans are unemployed", then "You've lost your house").
- **Lifelines:** A "Need cash?" sidebar offers to smash your kid's piggy bank ($16.23), donate plasma ($25) or take a payday loan ($50). This is the strongest mechanic in the game.
- **Payoff:** Survive the month or run out. A permanent "I can't do this" link is the exit, and the end leads to a donation page.
- **Replay:** Randomised events and different job paths.
- **Share:** The hook is a dare ("you'd never need help, right? PROVE IT"). There is no Wordle-style card. Sharing was mostly by word of mouth and classrooms (NGPF hosts it in its Arcade).
- **Mobile:** The game was made phone-playable in 2014, but in a 375px portrait viewport today the opening interstitial is cropped off the right edge.
- **Credibility:** The footer links a sources PDF.
- **Reach:** Over 4 million plays by almost 2 million people in 218 countries; average play about 9 minutes.
- **Sources:** https://playspent.org/html/ · https://www.planadviser.com/online-game-challenges-player-to-live-in-poverty/ · https://www.dandad.org/work/d-ad-awards-archive/spent · sources PDF https://playspent.org/html/files/sources/umd_spent_sources-2.pdf
- **Lesson for IFM:** This is the closest model for Broke by Friday-style games: a dare, one live balance, dilemmas, a result bubble, and lifelines with a hidden price.

### 2. NGPF Arcade (Next Gen Personal Finance) [played the index; game details secondhand]
- **Line-up (ngpf.org/arcade, 27 Oct 2026):** Every card states topic, a one-line hook and a time (5, 10, 15 or 20 min).
  - PAYBACK (college, 20 min)
  - Money Magic (help Enzo budget for Vegas, 15)
  - Bummer (insurance "Wheel of Bummers", 15)
  - Slick Wheels (car buying as the dealer, 10)
  - Influenc'd (entrepreneurship, 20)
  - STAX (investing, 20)
  - Credit Clash (card battle for a credit score, 15)
  - SPENT (10)
  - Shady Sam (loan shark, 10)
  - an Uber-driver career game (15)
  - Cat Insanity (credit, 5)
  - Crypto Craze (15)
- **Usage:** Over 10 million game plays in 2022. The most played that year, in order: STAX, Money Magic, Spent, Payback, Cat Insanity.
- **STAX:** Fits 20 years into about 20 minutes. Seven asset types (savings account, CD, index fund, individual stocks, bonds, commodities, gold), bull and bear markets, and mini-tutorials that pop up mid-game. There is an optional group leaderboard. Commentators describe it as rewarding patience and diversification.
- **Shady Sam / Slick Wheels:** Role reversal. You are the loan shark or the dealer, picking among three loan options per borrower, with immediate feedback on which was most profitable. The design write-ups argue that experiencing manipulation from the inside teaches the tactics. Awards: D&AD Wood Pencil (Shady Sam) and a 2026 MAIA award (Slick Wheels).
- **PAYBACK:** You enter a profile, choose a school type, housing and a job, and see the debt. Worked example: out-of-state $84,635 against in-state $11,317 on a replay. Webby winner. More than 100,000 students played it in one contest window.
- **Mobile and share:** Built for classrooms on Chromebooks, so sharing happens through teacher worksheets, not social cards.
- **Sources:** https://www.ngpf.org/arcade/ · https://ngpf.org/blog/best-of/ngpf-best-of-2022-ngpf-arcade-games · https://njcfe.org/a-new-stock-market-game-that-rewards-patience-and-diversification/ · https://www.ngpf.org/blog/just-one-resource/just-one-resource-interactive-shady-sam/ · https://www.maiawards.org/?p=5251 · https://www.ngpf.org/blog/paying-for-college/ngpf-launches-payback/

### 3. Visa Practical Money Skills: Financial Football / Financial Soccer [played the loader; mechanics secondhand]
- **Core loop:** Answer multiple-choice and true/false money questions. Right answers gain yards; wrong ones lose yards. Three age levels: Rookie (11–14), Pro (14–18), Hall of Fame (18+). Topics: budgeting, saving, credit, debt, identity theft and life events. Single-player or head-to-head.
- **Teaching:** Straight recall. The football is a skin over a quiz.
- **Localisation:** A FIFA-teams soccer edition (men's and women's teams, tournament mode). The National Bank of Georgia launched a localised version in 2026. Distributed to nearly 30,000 US schools.
- **Mobile and load:** The old practicalmoneyskills.com game URLs now return 404. The current web version (financialsoccer.com) loads a Unity WebGL build, and it was still on the loading bar after about 25 seconds on desktop. Apps exist for iOS and Android.
- **Sources:** https://usa.visa.com/about-visa/newsroom/press-releases.releaseId.15896.html · https://www.financialsoccer.com/ · https://nbg.gov.ge/en/media/news/financial-literacy-through-football-national-bank-of-georgia-and-visa-launch-financial-f
- **Lesson for IFM:** Localising the theme works; a quiz in costume and a heavy engine do not.

### 4. Zogo (US) [secondhand]
- **Core loop:** Bite-size modules, each about five tweet-length snippets plus a quiz, earning "pineapples" (points). Partners cite anywhere from about 400 to 800+ modules.
- **Payoff:** Points become real gift cards, or charity donations. Users level up through funny rank names (from "student of small change" to "currency conqueror"). There are referral points and daily trivia.
- **Access:** Through a partner credit union's access code. That suits a bank's marketing but is a hard gate for cold traffic.
- **Sources:** https://www.badcredit.org/news/zogo-helps-people-avoid-debt-via-gamified-education/ · https://thefinancialbrand.com/108201/gen-z-millennials-financial-literacy-mobile-app-personal-finance-education/ · https://mwafcu.org/resources/zogo.shtml
- **Lesson for IFM:** Funny rank names and tiny lessons are worth borrowing. Gift-card economics and access codes are not.

### 5. Duolingo mechanics: streaks, hearts, celebration animations [secondhand, partly from Duolingo's own blog]
- **Streaks (Duolingo's own figures):**
  - Learners who reach a 7-day streak are 3.6 times more likely to finish their course.
  - A milestone-day streak animation raised new learners' 7-day activity by 1.7%.
  - Letting people hold two streak freezes raised daily active learners by 0.38%.
  - Early days feel big: going from 2 to 3 days is a 50% jump.
- **Hearts:** Lose a heart per mistake, then you are locked out. A qualitative arXiv study describes this as punitive and as encouraging error avoidance.
- **Juice:** Heavy use of celebration animation, sound, a mascot and progress bars.
- **Sources:** https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/ · https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16175 · https://marishalakhiani.substack.com/p/breaking-down-duolingos-growth-model
- **Lesson for IFM:** Borrow the celebration and an optional daily ritual. Do not borrow lives that punish wrong answers, because wrong answers are where a money lesson lands.

### 6. BitLife (Candywriter) [secondhand: fan guides plus the app listing]
- **Core loop:** Tap "Age +1 year", then react to random life events. Every choice snowballs, and money (job, crime or inheritance) drives most options.
- **Payoff:** At death you get a life summary with net worth and one ribbon per life (Rich, Loaded, Mooch, Wasteful, Unlucky and so on), whichever single label sums up that life.
- **Replay:** Randomness plus collecting all the ribbons.
- **Share:** People screenshot their wild lives.
- **Mobile:** Native portrait app with one-thumb taps.
- **Sources:** https://primagames.com/tips/bitlife-ribbons-list-how-to-get-all-ribbons-in-bitlife · https://www.gfinityesports.com/bitlife/money/ · https://apps.apple.com/BR/app/id1374403536
- **Lesson for IFM:** "Age +1" is perfect for compounding games, and a single identity label at the end is very shareable.

### 7. Banzai (US schools) [secondhand]
- **Core loop:** Pre-test, then life scenarios, then a simulation (paychecks, bills, surprise expenses like parking tickets, interest and overdraft fees, with a savings goal), then a post-test.
- **Notable design:** Different students hit different random luck (the same cheap car breaks down for one player and not another).
- **Distribution:** Free via sponsoring credit unions; used by about 14,000 teachers.
- **Weakness for IFM's audience:** Opening with a pre-test.
- **Sources:** https://blog.banzai.org/its-a-real-life-game-banzai-program-teaches-lessons-in-financial-literacy/ · https://blog.banzai.org/game-lets-students-learn-real-life-money-lessons/

### 8. SIFMA Stock Market Game and Investopedia Simulator [secondhand]
- **SIFMA Stock Market Game:** Teams get a virtual $100,000 for 8–10 weeks, and the highest portfolio value wins. It needs teacher registration.
- **Investopedia Simulator:** $100,000 virtual cash, public and private games with rankings, and teachers can cap position sizes.
- **Weakness:** A short window plus a "highest value wins" rule rewards concentrated bets. Both also centre on picking named stocks, which IFM must not do.
- **Sources:** https://udel.edu/udaily/2024/july/ceee-stock-market-game-winners-honored · https://www.stockmarketgame.org/tour/ · https://www.mybanktracker.com/blog/investing/best-investment-simulators-302511 · https://wrdsclassroom.wharton.upenn.edu/investopedia-game_73473.html

### 9. Moneyopolis, Finny, BBC Bitesize money, ABC money games: not verified
- **Moneyopolis:** I could not find a live page or reliable coverage.
- **Finny:** Searches returned only "Finni – Learn Business", a different app.
- **BBC Bitesize and ABC Education:** No specific money game surfaced beyond KS1 coin-counting activities (and ABCmouse, which is unrelated).
- These are left out of the playbook rather than described from guesswork.
- **Search trail:** GoHenry Money Missions (points, badges and levels for ages 6–18) came up as the nearest kids' equivalent: https://www.gohenry.com/uk/blog/news/introducing-money-missions-for-financial-education-gohenry

## B. Indian examples

### 10. NSE World Investor Week games [secondhand: NSE pages and press releases]
- **Finance Learning Game:** Three word-puzzle levels (find a hidden term, guess a synonym, letter-by-letter). There were 50 winners a day for 7 days, each getting a Rs 500 ONDC voucher.
- **Scambush:** An interactive spot-the-scam game that awards a "Scam Pro" certificate.
- **Wheel of Financial Wisdom:** A spinning wheel of awareness questions with daily prizes.
- **Physical events:** SEBI and NSE also run quizzes at colleges, trade fairs (Goa, Aug 2026) and book fairs.
- **Sources:** https://www.nseindia.com/static/invest/world-investor-week-2025 · https://nsearchives.nseindia.com//web/pressrelease/2025-10/PR_cc_06102025_0_20251006221914.pdf · https://newsable.asianetnews.com/business/sebi-nse-promote-financial-literacy-at-goa-trade-fair-2026-articleshow-d545q5u
- **Lesson for IFM:** The scam theme is clearly a regulator priority. Prize-led and event-tied formats lapse once the week or the fair ends.

### 11. SEBI: Saa₹thi app and the "SEBI Check" QR game [secondhand]
- **Saa₹thi app:** Modules (markets, mutual funds, ETFs, grievance redress), calculators, a "Financial Health Checkup" question flow and a "Spot a scam" tool. Downloads reached 7.2 lakh by end-2025-26 (SEBI annual report). I found no evidence of a quiz.
- **Kolkata Book Fair 2026 stall:** Visitors scanned several QR codes and worked out which belonged to a genuinely SEBI-registered intermediary. It is a game that teaches a real action people can repeat at home. The stall passed 100,000 visitors.
- **Sources:** https://www.sebi.gov.in/reports-and-statistics/publications/aug-2026/Chapter%2008.pdf · https://en.vikaspedia.in/viewcontent/financial-education/securities-market/saa-thi-app-of-sebi?lgn=en · https://www.nism.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/SEBI-Investor-Awareness-Stall-at-IKBF-2026-Crosses-100000-Visitors-1.pdf
- **Lesson for IFM:** End scam games with a real, repeatable check (look the advisor up on SEBI's site), not just a score.

### 12. Zerodha Varsity app [secondhand: Zerodha's own launch post and app listing]
- **Core loop:** Each topic has three difficulty levels with a quiz each. Repeat attempts score fewer points per question, which nudges people to take the first try seriously.
- **Retention:** Daily goals and streaks.
- **Payoff:** A timed certification exam per module, which needs 60% on each quiz and 60% on the exam. The store listing mentions badges.
- **Mobile:** Native app.
- **Share:** The certificate.
- **Sources:** https://zerodha.com/z-connect/featured/introducing-the-varsity-mobile-app · https://apps.apple.com/in/app/varsity-by-zerodha/id1474610753
- **Lesson for IFM:** Indian learners respond to certificates and streaks, but this is a study app, not a 2-minute game.

### 13. Groww, Jar and CRED gamification [secondhand, mostly blogs]
- **Groww:** I found no official gamified-learning feature. One third-party pitch deck says it lacks deep gamification (unverified).
- **Jar:** Rounds UPI spends up to the nearest 10 and invests the change in digital gold. A "spin" reward is tied to saving, with a congratulatory notification.
- **CRED:** Coins equal to bills paid, spent on jackpots and draws, with a premium dark UI and "delightful" animations. Reviewers note that the jackpot's expected value is usually lower than the coins' value in the store.
- **Sources:** https://thehardcopy.co/starting-small-with-jar/ · https://www.plotline.so/blog/spin-the-wheel-gamification-mobile · https://www.markhub24.com/post/cred-gamified-rewards-exclusivity-architecture-and-credit-behavior-as-a-business-model
- **Lesson for IFM:** Indian fintech has made users used to spins, scratch cards and premium motion, so a flat, static IFM game will look dated next to them. Avoid the casino mechanics, which sit badly with an education brand.

### 14. Viral Indian money quizzes on Instagram: not verified
- Searches did not surface a documented viral Indian money-quiz format or reliable reach figures.
- Every result was creator-income blogs.
- **For IFM:** The KBC format (which IFM already uses in its quiz) is the strongest Indian quiz reference that needs no explaining. I would not claim a specific viral Instagram quiz without the account's own numbers.

## C. Craft references: short web games and explorable explanations

### 15. The Evolution of Trust (Nicky Case, 2017) [played]
- **Hook:** Stated time on the title card ("playing time: 30 min"), sound on/off, share icons (Facebook, Twitter, email) always visible bottom-right, about 40 fan translations, and a row of chapter-progress dots.
- **Core loop:** About 100 words of story (the 1914 Christmas truce, then "we suck at trust"), then "...let's play a game →". The first interaction is a single choice, Cheat or Cooperate, between two cartoon characters and a coin machine. Simulated players are then added one strategy type at a time, and the reader predicts and watches.
- **Payoff:** Three takeaways (repeated interaction, win-wins, low miscommunication), then a sandbox.
- **Mobile:** In a 375px portrait viewport the title card did not fit; it is a fixed landscape stage.
- **Sources:** https://ncase.me/trust/ · https://flowingdata.com/2017/07/27/trust · https://www.clearerthinking.org/post/evolution-of-trust-game

### 16. Parable of the Polygons (Vi Hart and Nicky Case, 2014) [played]
- **Core loop:** A scrolling "playable blog post". You drag unhappy shapes until everyone is happy, watch society segregate anyway, then a slider changes the bias, then the world starts already segregated.
- **Teaching:** Casual, joking voice, with the uncomfortable conclusion reached by your own hand.
- **Payoff:** Three numbered takeaways, a "big ol' sandbox", and footnotes to Schelling (1971) and Clark (1991).
- **Mobile:** A vertical scroll suits phones better than Trust's fixed stage.
- **Sources:** https://ncase.me/polygons/ · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parable_of_the_Polygons · https://www.killscreen.com/parable-polygons-teaches-us-danger-and-power-individual-bias/

### 17. NYT Upshot: "You Draw It" (2015) and "Is It Better to Rent or Buy?" (2014) [secondhand; nytimes.com is blocked from this browser and fetcher]
- **"You Draw It":**
  - Readers draw how college attendance changes with parental income.
  - Then the true line appears (nearly linear, about +0.7 points per income percentile), with other readers' guesses shaded behind it.
  - Hundreds of thousands drew one, and many got it wrong.
- **Rent vs Buy:**
  - 20+ adjustable inputs (price, rate, down payment, growth rates, taxes, costs).
  - The answer is a single break-even figure. My description of the per-slider mini charts and the exact answer sentence comes from memory of the piece and could not be re-checked today.
- **Sources:** https://flowingdata.com/2015/05/28/compare-your-curve-to-reality-for-income-percentile-versus-college-attendance/ · https://www.r-bloggers.com/2015/05/chances-of-going-to-college-based-on-parents-income/ · https://www.ngpf.org/blog/interactive/is-it-better-to-rent-or-buy-a-financial-calculator/ · https://brickunderground.com/blog/2014/05/renting_smarter_than_buying

### 18. The Pudding: "How Bad Is Your Spotify" (2020) and "Where in the USA is this?" [secondhand]
- **"How Bad Is Your Spotify":** A deadpan AI "judges" your taste and gives you a named flavour of bad. It hit #1 trending on Twitter as people shared screenshots, and drew millions of views before its official launch.
- **"Where in the USA is this?":** A Wordle-meets-GeoGuessr daily game.
- **House style:** The interaction is the journalism, and the reader reaches the conclusion before being told it.
- **Sources:** https://mikelacher.com/work/how-bad-is-your-spotify · https://musictech.com/news/how-bad-is-your-spotify/ · https://pudding.cool/faq/ · https://webiano.digital/the-pudding-proves-that-a-website-can-still-be-journalism/
- **Lesson for IFM:** A gentle, funny verdict is very shareable. With money, keep it flattering, because a verdict on someone's taste is fun but a verdict on their finances can sting.

### 19. Wordle (Josh Wardle, 2021; NYT since 2022) [secondhand on growth]
- **Core loop:** One puzzle a day, the same for everyone; six guesses with colour feedback; no account.
- **Payoff:** A stats panel with streak and guess distribution, plus a countdown to tomorrow.
- **Share:** The emoji grid "just leaves the colours", so it is spoiler-free. It was invented by New Zealand players typing coloured squares and then automated by Wardle. Growth went from 90 players in November to more than 2 million.
- **Why it travels:** It works as bragging or commiseration, and every grid is a free ad.
- **Sources:** https://thespinoff.co.nz/media/13-01-2022/those-coloured-boxes-on-twitter-are-new-zealands-fault · https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/heres-why-the-word-game-wordle-went-viral-180979439/ · https://www.buzzfeednews.com/article/stefficao/how-wordle-went-viral-strategy

## D. Supporting references (mechanics, not games)

### 20. Spotify Wrapped
- Data turned into an identity, on 9:16 cards sized for Stories.
- Top songs are framed as a quiz and top artists as a race.
- The design has to make the user look good, or nobody shares it.
- Source: https://nogood.io/blog/spotify-wrapped-marketing-strategy/ · https://adma.com.au/resources/personal-shareable-no-strings-attached-the-winning-formula-behind-spotify-wrapped

### 21. Sorted money-personality quiz (New Zealand, with AUT)
- 17 research-based questions, with results shown as tarot-style cards placing you on traits.
- Source: https://retirement.govt.nz/news/latest-news/sorted-and-aut-collaborate-to-create-money-personality-quiz-driven-by-new-nz-research

### 22. "Juice it or lose it" (Jonasson and Purho, GDC Europe 2012)
- Tweening and easing, squash-and-stretch and screen shake turn a flat Breakout clone into something that feels alive.
- Counterpoint talk: "Don't Juice It or Lose It".
- Source: https://roblog.co.uk/2024/03/juicy-games/ · https://rpgplayground.com/research-making-a-juicy-game/

- Absolute URL, with the meta tags inside the first 300KB of the HTML.

- Kids' in-app money lessons with points, badges and levels, mapped to the UK and US curricula.

## Cross-cutting observations

1. **The best teaching games make you choose, not recall.** SPENT, STAX, Shady Sam, PAYBACK and the Nicky Case pieces all teach through a decision followed by a visible consequence. Financial Football and most app quizzes teach through recall.
2. **Classroom-distributed games rarely have a share mechanic.** That is the NGPF and Banzai model. The games that spread by themselves (Wordle, Spotify Wrapped, How Bad Is Your Spotify) all end on an identity or a spoiler-free result made for posting. IFM's traffic comes from social, so IFM needs the second kind.
3. **Phone handling is the weak spot across the whole field.** Two of the five games I opened broke at portrait phone width on the first screen, and a third (Financial Football) was still loading a game engine after about 25 seconds. A fast, portrait-first IFM game is a real edge, not a nice-to-have.
4. **Indian incumbents (NSE and SEBI) are scam-focused, event-tied and prize-led.** No one owns an always-on, 2-minute, mum-friendly money game with a WhatsApp-native share. That gap is IFM's opening.
