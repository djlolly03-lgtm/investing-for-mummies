What to do with this

Nine moves the data supports

Ordered by how much the responses point at them. The first three are revenue; the rest are hygiene that makes the next report sharper.

New product

Launch "IFM Level 2": wills, trusts and tax, then balance sheets

Evidence: 10 asked for depth, 5 named wills/trusts/tax, 5 named balance sheets, 19 ticked Guest Lecturer, 17 ticked Refresher. Nikita asked for a Budget-day class.

Do: two half-day sessions, priced as add-ons, offered first to the 32 respondents by name. A guest CA covers tax; Hiral covers balance sheets using a company the class already knows. Run the Budget-day session on 1 February.

Community

Open the alumni WhatsApp group this week

Evidence: 13 of the 15 people asked said "Yes, add me!". Hetal asked for it unprompted. The dashboard shows 0 because of the column shift.

Do: one group for all batches, Hiral posts one news-led explainer a week (the reel topics already in the pipeline). It is also the channel that sells Level 2 and fills October B2's 5 empty seats by referral.

Marketing

Harvest 16 ready testimonials, then ask the other 15

Evidence: every mummy asked up to May said "happy to be quoted". The question was dropped from the form in August, so nobody from Aug or Sep has been asked.

Do: put the quotes on this page straight onto the website and proposals, credited by first name and batch. Send the 15 August and September respondents a one-line WhatsApp asking for consent. Put the question back on the form.

Fix now · response rate

Collect feedback in the room, on the last day

Evidence: the teens hit 30% in two minutes with the form on their phones in class. Mummies, chased by WhatsApp afterwards, sit at 31% after six months, and April is at 4 of 15.

Do: the last 10 minutes of session 8 is the form, QR on screen. Target 80%. Still chase April's 11 pending now while they remember the course.

Measurement

Replace "would you recommend" with a 0 to 10 score

Evidence: 31 of 32 picked the top option. That cannot tell you which batch, which day or which topic landed worse.

Do: NPS question plus one line: "which session was the hardest to follow?". Then batch-to-batch comparison becomes meaningful.

Product

Sell the book as its own thing, and make a digital edition

Evidence: 12 people named the book unprompted as the best part. Urvi and Shruti SD asked for a digital version.

Do: a PDF edition for alumni, and a paid standalone for people who will not do the course. It is the most-praised asset you have and it currently exists only as a classroom handout.

Pace

Offer an optional ninth session, not a slower course

Evidence: 2 asked for more time (one said "we might need 10 sessions"), 1 asked for less detail on mutual funds, 7 said it was perfect. The pace is right for most.

Do: keep 8 sessions. Add an optional "bring your statements" clinic after session 8 for those who want the hands-on step that Shilpa and Prachi asked for.

Teens

Lead teen marketing with the stock game and Broke by Friday

Evidence: 10 of 13 teens said stocks interested them most; the two games take 11 of 13 favourite-game votes; 12 of 13 would come back.

Do: the IYA and IFT flyers open with the game, not the syllabus. Answer the allowance question in the course: "you got ₹5,000 for Diwali, now what?"

Parked idea

A basic course for seniors

Evidence: one ask (Ashima). Not a signal yet.

Do: nothing now. Add a "who else in your family should do this?" question to the form and let the data decide.

Wendy: the role, the pipeline, the first 90 days

Built from the 17 August role review, the Enrolment & Growth JD (18 August) and the day-one conversation on 15 October. For the Monday 19 October sit-down.

Where she sits

Three lanes, hard boundaries

Follow-ups belong to nobody else any more. Wendy owns everything between an enquiry arriving and the money clearing.

LaneOwnerBoundaryThe job
BroadcastSakshiEverything before interestGet IFM in front of people. Fill the top of the funnel. Measured in followers, not seats.
SalesWendyInterest → paymentFollow up, pitch, close. Owns the pipeline, the waitlist, Zoho and attribution. The only role measured in rupees.
Retention & OpsAsbaPayment → completion and beyondEverything after the swipe: logistics, invoicing, certificates, feedback, testimonials.

The two handoffs define the job. Sakshi passes every enquiry to Wendy the same day it arrives and does not chase it. When payment clears, the auto-email fires and the student is Asba's. Wendy lives entirely in between.

Scope

What is hers, and what is not

Wendy owns

  • The pipeline. Every prospect from first interest to payment. Nobody else touches it.
  • The backlog. The prospects pending on the admin dashboard. Warm, and ageing.
  • All fresh leads. Comments, DMs, enquiries from Sakshi, Meta-ad near-misses, and the registration-page waitlist.
  • Zoho Bigin. The CRM. If the data is wrong, that is on her.
  • Closing. Calls, WhatsApp, objections, follow-through to payment.
  • Attribution. Coupon codes and source tagging. The coupon field already exists on the registration page.
  • Individual buyers across all three programmes. A mum for herself, a parent for a teen (IFT), a young adult for themselves (IYA).

Wendy does not touch

  • Past students and Hiral's network. The alumni came through Hiral personally. A call from a stranger costs more than a seat. Spot an opportunity, tell Hiral, do not call.
  • Institutional sales. Schools, colleges, corporates. Long-cycle, relationship-led. Stays with Hiral and Asba, opportunistically, no target.
  • Content, Stories, Instagram, LinkedIn. Sakshi's.
  • Anything after payment. Batch logistics, scheduling, invoicing, feedback. Asba's.
  • Curriculum, pricing, teaching, dates. Hiral's.
  • Partner referrals (Dezerv). Hiral's relationship; students are told she earns a referral.

The line is drawn by the buyer, not the product. A parent enrolling one teen is Wendy's sale. A school buying thirty IFT seats is not.

The plan

First 90 days

Days 1 to 2 · done

Sit in the room

15 and 16 October: watch the live classes. Hear the fear and the objections first-hand before touching a phone.

Days 3 to 14

Work the backlog

Call every pending prospect. Not email. Find out why each one did not convert. This teaches the objections faster than any briefing and should produce the first seats.

Days 15 to 45

Build the fresh-lead motion

Every enquiry from Sakshi reaches her the same day. Set the response-time standard, the follow-up cadence, the objection handling. Speed is the whole advantage.

Days 46 to 90

Make it repeatable

Zoho clean and current. Written cadence. Attribution codes live. Inbound converting at a rate that can be measured.
