Locked-off top-down drone shot, camera perfectly still, looking straight down at a stone promenade in South Mumbai at golden hour, long warm shadows. Along the road at the top of frame black-and-yellow taxis keep driving past throughout. A single tall banner lies on the pavers folded in half along its length like a closed book, so it looks like one thin dark strip down the centre. The fold is the spine and the spine stays on the ground along the centre line for the whole shot. Eight women in ivory, cream and soft teal, four on each side, walk toward the strip in sync, stop, bend and take hold of the free edge on their side, then walk outward away from the centre in unison, lifting that edge and laying it down flat. The two halves hinge open from the spine like book pages, always joined at the centre, one continuous piece of fabric with no gap ever visible between the halves. Realistic cloth physics, fabric rippling and settling, edges catching the low sun, until the whole banner lies flat and taut filling the frame, exactly as in the final frame, the print crisp and unchanged, four women along each long edge. Calm, precise choreography. No camera movement, no zoom. Street ambience, passing traffic, footsteps, a deep whoosh of heavy fabric unfurling. No music.
