# How to present this — Wednesday 19 August

**Room:** Hiral · Ketaki (Head of HR) · Asba · Sakshi
**Length:** 45 minutes
**What this is:** an **alignment exercise.** Not a hiring approval, not a strategy pitch.

Everyone in this room has been working hard on the same business without ever seeing the same picture of it. Hiral teaching, Asba operating, Sakshi posting — all against a plan that lived in one head. That's the actual problem being solved tomorrow. The hire is one box in the picture, not the point of the meeting.

> **The test of whether it worked isn't whether anything is approved. It's whether all four of them can describe the plan in a sentence on Thursday morning — and whether Asba knows the SOP is hers without being asked.**

**Which changes the format.** Alignment is not produced by a good presentation; it's produced by people saying it back in their own words. Talk for less time than feels right, and leave room for them to argue.

---

## The one thing that could go wrong

**Do not run individual role reviews in a group.**

Two conversations in this pack are personal: Asba's *"sales isn't your forte"*, and Sakshi's targets, ramp and October review. **Delivered in front of HR and each other, both land as public performance management** — even said kindly.

| Conversation | Where it happens |
|---|---|
| The org model, the vacancy, the numbers | **The group meeting** |
| Asba's lane and the withdrawn leads target | ✅ Already done 1:1 on 17 Aug |
| Sakshi's outbound targets, ramp, October review | ⚠️ **Must happen 1:1 first — see below** |

She has been in post since July, she's junior and soft-spoken, and tomorrow she'll be handed a new hard habit and a set of numbers in front of the HR head. That's not fair, and a nervous person will not ask the questions she needs to ask.

4. **Say the 19 October date**, and that title and pay are reviewed then.

Then in the group meeting she is hearing a confirmation, not a verdict.

## Running order — 45 minutes, and only 25 of them are you talking

### 1 · Own the absence first *(5 min)*
Never open on an org chart — people read boxes as politics before they've heard why. And open by taking the gap yourself, which frees everyone else to engage instead of defending their patch.

> *"We've all been working hard on the same thing without ever writing down what the thing is. That's my gap, not yours. So today I want to show you the whole picture — where we are, what the numbers actually say, and where we're going — and then you'll see why the one empty box is empty."*

> *"We're good at the teaching. 116 students, ₹15.65L, and the people who come through stay. What we're bad at is two things — being found, and following up with people who put their hand up."*

### 2 · The evidence *(8 min)*
Four facts. Let them sit. Don't soften them, and don't attach names.

| | |
|---|---|
| **356 followers** | 344 a week ago — the Stories push is already moving it |
| **Engagement is above the competitor median** | 4.7 comments per 100 likes vs 3.3 — **the content is not the problem** |
| **0 of 12 posts** | contained any ask that leads to a class |
| **27 prospects** | raised their hand and nobody followed up |
| **99 of 116 students** | never asked for feedback |

**The line that lands:** *"We are not short of interest. We're short of anyone whose job it is to catch it."*

### 3 · The model *(7 min)* — **put up the org chart here**
Three lanes. Boundary is the payment.

**Spend most of this slot on the empty middle box.** That box is the entire argument, and it's what exonerates everyone.

> *"Two lanes are staffed. The middle one never existed. It got smeared across people who didn't want it and didn't take upside from it — so it's nobody's fault that it didn't get done."*

### 4 · What it means for each person *(7 min)*
**Role level only. No performance, no targets, no dates.**

- **Sakshi — Broadcast.** Stories, outbound, filming, creatives, LinkedIn support. *Brings people to the door.*
- **Asba — Retention & Ops.** Everything from payment onward, plus the SOP. **Say clearly: the 2-leads-a-month target is withdrawn.**
- **Vacant — Sales.** The hire.
- **Hiral — teaching, curriculum, the brand.**

### 5 · The hire *(8 min)* — doc 5
- Recommend **"Enrolment & Growth Executive"**, not "Sales Head" — no team to head, and a Head's salary is most of a year's revenue
- ₹25–40k fixed + ₹750–1,200 per seat, **uncapped**
- **Break-even is roughly two seats a month**, against a cycle of 36–40. That number does more work than any argument.
- Assessment is a **live mock call**, not an interview

### 6 · The money already on the table *(5 min)*
**Close here. It's the most optimistic thing you have and it should be the last thing they hear.**

> *"We have 17 unsold September seats right now, and 27 people who asked about a course and never heard back. That's roughly ₹3.2L sitting in inventory we've already built. It doesn't need Instagram, the agency, or anything we're building. It needs someone to make the calls."*

### 7 · The alignment round *(10 min — the most important part)*

**Stop presenting. Ask three questions and let them talk.**

**a) "Say back what you own, in one sentence."** Thirty seconds each, round the room. Sakshi, Asba, Ketaki, then you.

> **If any of the four sentences comes out fuzzy, that's the part of the plan that isn't real yet.** Far better to find it tomorrow than in November. Don't rescue a fuzzy answer by re-explaining — ask them to try again in their own words.

**b) "Where does this break?"** They know the handoffs better than you do. Asba knows what happens when someone wants to switch batches; Sakshi knows what a DM at 11pm looks like. The plan is a hypothesis until they've stress-tested it.

**c) "What worries you about this?"** — and mean it.

> **If nobody disagrees with anything, you haven't aligned — you've broadcast.** A room that nods at everything on Wednesday raises it privately in October. Push once: *"genuinely, what's the bit you're not sure about?"*

**Then, briefly, the open items.** The four decisions below are mostly confirmations — treat them as a two-minute wrap, not the centrepiece.

### One consequence of A worth stating plainly

Choosing beginners-only means **the 72 mums who have already been through the course cannot buy anything.** That doesn't shrink cohort #1 — the 12–24 seats we expect from warm contacts never counted them (a separate number from the 36–40 a full cycle holds). What it forgoes is a *second* revenue line worth roughly **₹1.6–2.6L** from an advanced cohort.

It also changes what those 72 people are for: they become a **referral and testimonial pool, not a purchase pool.** The sales hire's alumni motion is built on that basis, and 99 of the 116 have never even been asked for feedback — so there's real value there regardless.

**An advanced course is the obvious second product whenever Hiral has bandwidth.** Worth planting tomorrow, not deciding.

## The open items — the detail behind each

**Take them in this order.** Decision 1 is first on the list but second in the room, because **the cohort calendar blocks two of the other three.** Without dates and seat counts you cannot set a sales target, cannot publish the JD, and cannot build the course page. Start there.

Each decision below has a question phrased so it **cannot be answered with "let's think about it."** The failure mode of a meeting like this is four polite nods and no artefacts.

---

### DECISION A · The cohort calendar *(take this first)*
**Decides:** Hiral · **Blocks:** the JD, the course page, every sales target

**How it already works:** two parallel batches per cycle — **Mon/Wed** and **Tue/Thu**. Dates are published on a **rolling two-month window**, because mums do not commit three to five months ahead. September and October went out by the first Friday of August; November goes out by the first Friday of September.

**That ritual is right and shouldn't change.** What's missing isn't a longer horizon — it's that the ritual isn't written down, owned, or visible to the people who need to sell against it.

**Ask exactly this:**
> *"How many seats in each batch, are the September and October dates actually published, and who owns hitting the first-Friday deadline every month?"*

**Three things to pin down:**

| | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Seats per batch** | **18–20 per batch once the new room is ready, so 36–40 seats a cycle.** Every sales target is a fraction of that number — confirm the exact figure so it stops being a range. |
| **Are Sept + Oct actually live?** | The first Friday of August was the 7th. By your own rule those dates should already be out. **If they aren't, that's the finding** — a good ritual that isn't happening. |
| **Who owns the first Friday?** | This belongs to Asba — batch calendar sits in her lane. It should be a named, recurring item in her SOP, not something remembered. |

**Decided when:** seats-per-batch is stated out loud, Sept/Oct are confirmed live, and the first-Friday publish has a named owner and a place in the SOP.

**Why this matters more than a long calendar:** the sales hire doesn't need six months of dates. They need **the next two months, always, without asking.** A rolling window that reliably refreshes is worth more than a six-month plan that goes stale.

### DECISION B · Approve the hire — title, band, incentive
**Decides:** HR + Hiral *(budget)* · **Blocks:** everything in the sales lane

**Ask exactly this:**
> *"Do we approve one full-time Enrolment & Growth Executive at ₹25–40k fixed, no incentive until 20 enrolments, who owns hiring it, and what's the start-by date?"*

**Four things to settle, not one:**

| | | Position |
|---|---|---|
| **Title** | Enrolment & Growth Executive vs Sales Head | **Enrolment & Growth Executive.** No team to head; a Head's salary is most of a year's revenue |
| **Fixed** | The band | ₹25–40k/month. **Pitch at the top of it** — it's the whole package for now |
| **Incentive** | When | **Not until 20 students have enrolled on the main course.** We don't yet know our conversion rate, so any number today is a guess |
| **Hiring owner** | Who runs the process, by when | Name a person and a start-by date in the room |

**On the no-incentive decision:** the rationale is genuinely strong and should be said out loud — *"after 20 enrolments we'll know what a seat costs to win, and we can design something real from data."* Expect HR to flag that fixed-only narrows the candidate pool. That's true; the answer is to pitch at ₹35–40k and give the trigger a rough timeline.

**The number that wins the argument:** break-even is **roughly two seats a month** against a cycle of 36–40. And 27 people are already waiting.

**Decided when:** headcount is approved in writing, with a band, an incentive trigger, a named hiring owner and a start-by date.

### DECISION C · B2C or institutional — what does this hire actually sell?
**Decides:** Hiral · **Blocks:** the JD wording, and the incentive design

**Ask exactly this:**
> *"Does this person touch schools and corporates at all — and if not, who owns institutional, with what target?"*

**Recommendation: B2C seats only.** Institutional selling is long-cycle, multi-stakeholder and relationship-led; converting a warm mum by phone is short-cycle and emotional. **People excellent at one are frequently poor at the other**, and per-seat commission doesn't work for a six-month school deal.

**The honest wrinkle to name out loud:** if institutional stays with Hiral and Asba, that is **sales work sitting in the retention lane** — a deliberate exception to the model, not an oversight. Say so, so the model doesn't quietly erode. Asba has already built the pitch deck, so she is the natural interim owner.

**Decided when:** one line is written into the JD, and institutional has a named owner.

## What not to say

- ❌ **"You've stagnated."** That was a 1:1 remark. It does not belong in a group.
- ❌ Anything implying the 27 unworked prospects are Asba's failure. They're the empty lane's failure.
- ❌ Sakshi's trial, review or pay in front of others.
- ❌ **"Nobody's been asking students what's working."** True, but it reads as a shot at Hiral's teaching. See below.

## Three moments that could go sideways

**"Are you saying my curriculum is wrong?"**
No — and get ahead of it. Frame it as a *measurement* gap, not a teaching one:
> *"You found out the insurance session was dry because you were in the room and you noticed. That shouldn't depend on you noticing. If we ask after every session, the data tells us — and you get to make the call with evidence instead of instinct."*
**She keeps full authorship. She just gets better information.**

**"Can we afford another hire?"**
Break-even is roughly two seats a month against a cycle of 36–40. And the 27 unworked prospects are sitting there *now* — arguably it costs more not to hire.

**"Why can't Sakshi just do the follow-ups?"**
She can log and hand over — she should not chase and close. Different skill, and pressure-selling to nervous women damages the brand more than a missed seat costs. Interim is a stopgap, not a plan.
