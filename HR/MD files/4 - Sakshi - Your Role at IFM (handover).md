## Why this role exists

IFM teaches money to women who were never taught it. We're good at the teaching part — our workshops work, our games work, and the people who find us stay.

**The problem is that not enough people find us.**

Right now IFM has 356 Instagram followers, growing by about one and a half a day. **The target is 1% a day** — about three and a half today, but the number climbs as the base does. Held for a year, 1% a day takes us past 10,000. Here's the interesting part: **the people who do see our content engage with it more than most of our competitors' audiences engage with theirs.** The content isn't the problem. There just aren't enough people seeing it.

**Your job is to change that number.** Everything below serves one goal:

> ### Fill IFM's classes.

Not likes. Not follower counts for their own sake. Seats in classes, filled by people who found us because you put us in front of them.

# BLOCK 1 · Outbound Engagement — 75 min

## What this is

Leaving genuine comments on **other people's** posts, so their audience discovers IFM.

## Why it's the most important thing you do

Nobody is finding IFM by searching for it. Posting more doesn't fix that — we already post, and the people who see it like it.

The only free way to get in front of new people is to show up where they already are. **This block is the entire growth strategy.** Everything else you do keeps the audience we have. This is the only thing that adds to it.

## How the 75 minutes runs

| Time | What you're doing |
|---|---|
| **0–10 min** | Open the **Today** tab. Scan the queue and skip the duds *(see below)*. |
| **10–50 min** | Work the queue — **15 comments**, about 2½ minutes each. |
| **50–65 min** | Go back to **yesterday's** comments on Instagram. Anyone replied? Reply again. |
| **65–75 min** | Buffer. If it's flowing, keep going. If it isn't, stop. |

**Don't skip that 50–65 slot.** Everyone does, and it's where the follows actually come from. A comment is an introduction — the reply to their reply is the conversation.

**And it's much less work than it sounds:** of 15 comments, typically only three or four get a reply. So "yesterday's replies" is three or four conversations, not fifteen. It's the *best* fifteen minutes of your day — you're talking to people who already responded to you.

## You don't start from a blank box — the comments are drafted for you

**Each card in the queue comes with a suggested comment**, written for that specific post after someone has actually studied it. It's in the hub *(and until that's built, in `content/outbound-drafts.json`)*.

Each one gives you:

| | |
|---|---|
| **What the post says** | A one-line gist, so you know what you're walking into |
| **The suggested comment** | Ready to copy |
| **Why it works** | The reasoning — this is the part that trains you |
| **A priority** | **HIGH** = recent post, small like-count, your comment stays near the top · **LOW** = big account, it'll sink · **SKIP** = deliberately don't comment, with the reason |

### The loop
> read the gist → **Open ↗** → **check the post actually matches the gist** → copy → **change a few words so it's yours** → post → **✓ Commented**

### Three rules that matter

1. **Open the post before you use the draft.** The draft is written from the cover image. A reel's voiceover can go somewhere the cover doesn't — if the post isn't what the gist says, adapt it or skip it. **Posting a comment that doesn't match the post is worse than not commenting.**
2. **Always change a few words.** Make it sound like you. The day someone replies *"did a bot write this?"* is the day this whole channel stops working.
3. **Respect the SKIPs.** They're there for a reason — usually that we've already commented on that account's series this week. Commenting on four posts from the same series looks like stalking, not engagement.

**Do the HIGH ones first.** If you only get through half the queue, you want the half where the comment is actually visible.

If a draft doesn't feel right to you, write your own — you're in the comment section every day and you'll spot things the drafts miss. Tell Hiral when that happens; it makes the next batch better.

## Work in batches of five

Don't ping-pong between Instagram and the hub fifteen times — the app-switching is what makes this feel like a grind.

> **Open five cards → comment on all five → come back and tap ✓ on all five.**

Three switches instead of fifteen.

## ⚠️ Check two things before you comment

The queue is automatic, which means **it will sometimes hand you a post that isn't worth your time.** Two quick checks before you open anything:

| Check the card for | Skip it if | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **The date** | It's more than a few days old | Nobody reads comments on old posts. A 2022 post is a dead end — your comment will never be seen by anyone. |
| **The like count** | It's in the thousands (♥ 29K, ♥ 80K) | Your comment lands somewhere around number 400. It's invisible. |

**The sweet spot is a recent post with a few hundred likes.** There you'll be near the top and actually get read.

You'll see both kinds in the queue right now — a Rachana Ranade reel from 2022 with 80,000 likes is exactly what to skip. **Skipping is free.** Never feel you have to work through a card just because it's there. Hiral knows about this and it's being improved; until then, use your judgement.

If you're skipping so many that you can't reach 15, tell Hiral — that's a queue problem, not a you problem.

## What a good comment looks like

> **@somemumcreator posts:** *"Why you need an emergency fund before you invest"*
>
> ❌ *"Great post! 🔥"*
> ❌ *"So true 💯 Follow us for more money tips ✨"*
> ✅ *"The 6-month rule is where most mums get stuck — on one income it sounds impossible, so people don't start at all. We tell them to put in ₹500 and stop calling it a failure. Starting badly beats not starting."*

The good one works because it **adds something the post didn't say**, it's **specific enough to prove you actually read it**, and it **sounds like a person with an opinion** rather than a brand being nice.

Nobody follows an account because it said "great post." They follow because it said something interesting.

### The test
Before you post it, read it back: **could this comment sit under any other post?** If yes, it isn't specific enough. Rewrite it.

### Use AI to help you draft these
You don't have to invent fifteen original thoughts from nothing every morning. Paste the post into Claude, ask for two or three comment options in IFM's voice, then **pick one and rewrite it in your own words.**

Never paste an AI reply straight in — it reads like one. Use it to get past the blank screen, not to do the thinking.

## ✅ DO

- **Comment on posts under 48 hours old.** Older posts are dead — nobody reads those comments.
- **Target mid-size accounts (5,000–50,000 followers).** A comment under a 68,000-like post is invisible. Under a 400-like post, you're the top comment.
- **Reply to anyone who replies to you.** Every single time.
- **In Facebook mum groups, just answer questions genuinely** — don't mention IFM unless someone asks. People click your profile anyway, and it works far better than a pitch.
- **Spread them out.** Twenty-five comments in ten minutes looks automated.
- **Log each one as you go**, not at the end of the day.

## ❌ DON'T

- **Never pitch. Never drop a link.** Not once. It reads as spam and gets our comments hidden.
- **Never leave the same comment twice** — not even reworded.
- **Never comment on a competitor's sales or launch post.** It looks like poaching.
- **Never correct or argue with anyone publicly**, even when they're wrong. If it's genuinely harmful money advice, screenshot it for Hiral instead.
- **Don't chase the big creators.** It feels productive and achieves nothing.
- **Don't do all 25 at 11pm** to hit the number. A log full of midnight timestamps tells its own story.

## What progress looks like

**Week 1:** 8 a day, and they take too long. That's completely fine.
**Week 4:** 12–15 a day, replies starting to come back regularly.
**Week 8:** a steady 15 a day that you could keep up indefinitely, and you know which accounts actually return follows — so that's where your time goes.

# BLOCK 2 · Stories — 50 min

## What this is

3–5 Instagram Stories a day. **These are completely yours. No approval needed. Experiment hard.**

## Why it matters

The feed is where people *discover* IFM. **Stories are where they get to know us** — and the people watching your Stories are the warm ones, the ones who might actually take a seat in a class.

Stories also disappear after 24 hours, which is exactly why you should take risks with them. One that flops costs nothing. One that works tells us what to make more of.

## The daily shape

Don't stare at a blank screen. Most days look like this:

| # | Story | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **A question** | Poll or question sticker. Ask something real. |
| 2 | **Something real** | Behind the scenes. A class. Hiral prepping. |
| 3 | **Teach one tiny thing** | 15 seconds. One idea — not a lesson. |
| 4 | **Respond** | Share back a reply, a poll result, a DM *(with permission)*. |
| 5 | **The ask** | One call to action. Once a day, not five times. |

Post them **across the day** — morning, afternoon, evening — not all five at 9pm.

## Tag our past students — this is a priority

We have **116 people who have already been through an IFM class.** Tagging them in Stories is one of the most valuable things you can do, and it costs nothing.

**Why it works so well:**

- The tagged person gets a notification and usually looks — you've re-engaged an alumna with zero effort
- **Many of them re-share it to their own Story** — which puts IFM in front of *their* followers, who are mostly other mums. That's exactly the audience we want, arriving warm and free
- It makes people feel recognised, which is why they re-share in the first place

At 356 followers, **one alumna with 800 followers re-sharing your story reaches more new people than a week of posting.**

### How to do it

| | |
|---|---|
| **Target** | **8–10 different students tagged per week** |
| **Rotate** | Work through the student list — don't tag the same few people repeatedly |
| **Good moments to tag** | Class footage they appear in · a throwback to their session · sharing a win or a question they asked · a poll result their class inspired |
| **Log it** | Students tagged, and how many re-shared |

With 116 students and 8–10 a week, you'll cycle through everyone roughly every three months. That's the right rhythm — frequent enough to matter, never enough to feel spammed.

### ⚠️ Ask permission first

**Money is personal.** Being publicly tagged as attending a finance class isn't something everyone wants — it can feel like admitting they didn't know something, and for some it touches family privacy.

- **Ask before the first time you tag someone.** A quick DM: *"We'd love to tag you in a story from your class — happy for us to?"*
- **Once someone says yes, you don't need to ask again.** Keep a list of who has agreed.
- **If they say no, or don't reply, don't tag them.** No follow-up, no chasing.
- **Never tag anyone in something that reveals their money situation** — what they earn, what they owe, what they didn't know.

## Certificate day — the sequence worth repeating

Every batch ends with certificates. **That day is the best Story material we get**, and here's why it works: a promo says *"come to our workshop."* A photo of someone holding a certificate says *"this finishes, and people leave with something."* That's proof, not a promise.

And remember who's watching: **our followers are mums.** A mum seeing teenagers holding investing certificates doesn't think *"nice for them"* — she thinks *"my kid should have this."*

**The sequence, same day:**
1. The group photo
2. One teaching moment from the session
3. A poll — *"Would your teen pass a money quiz?"*
4. The ask — **DM us TEENS** *(then log every DM as an enquiry)*

**⚠️ Before posting anything with teens in it:** get a parent's yes first. These are children, their names are printed on the certificates, and the workshops run on pre-registration so parents are one WhatsApp message away. Ask, keep a note of who said yes, and make sure names aren't readable in the shot. **No consent, no post — no exceptions.**

## Use the games — they're our best hook

Other accounts pull people in with quizzes — *"what's your money personality"*. **We have eleven actual games, which is a far stronger version of the same idea, and we barely mention them.**

Put a game in Stories regularly: a clip of the screen, a poll about it, then *"DM us PLAY and we'll send it to you."* Interactive beats informative — nobody screenshots a tip, but they will play a game and tell a friend.

There are already good reels about the games sitting unused. Ask for them.

## The single best trick

**Share your poll results back the next day.**

> *"62% of you said you've never checked what your money is actually earning. Let's fix that today 👇"*

It costs nothing, it makes people feel heard, and it gives you tomorrow's content for free. Do this every week.

## ✅ DO

- **Reply to every story reply.** A story reply *is* a DM — that's a conversation started, which is the whole point.
- **Tag past students, 8–10 different ones a week** *(with their permission — see above)*.
- **When someone re-shares your story, thank them in a DM.** It takes ten seconds and it makes the next re-share far more likely.
- **Use polls to learn, not to entertain.** "What's your biggest money worry?" is worth ten "Guess which is cheaper 🤔" quizzes.
- **Film vertical. Always.**
- **Show faces** — yours, Hiral's, the class. People follow people.
- **Keep text big enough to read without zooming.**

## ❌ DON'T

- **Don't post 15 stories.** People tap through and stop watching. Five good ones beat fifteen fillers.
- **Don't tag someone who hasn't agreed**, and don't tag the same handful of students every week.
- **Don't just re-share the agency's feed post.** That's duplication. *Extend* it — add a thought, run a poll about it, show a behind-the-scenes clip.
- **Don't let a poll die unanswered.** If you ask, come back with the result.
- **Don't wait until you have something polished.** Polished isn't what performs here.

# THE FIRST-HOUR RITUAL · every new IFM post

## What this is

**Every time IFM's feed gets a new post, the first hour belongs to you.** There are around 20 women in the office. Walk the floor and get real conversations going under the post while it's fresh.

## Why this matters so much

Instagram decides how far a post travels based on **what happens in its first hour.** A post that gets 8–10 real comments quickly gets shown to far more people; a post that sits silent gets buried. Right now our posts average 1.4 comments — mostly arriving too late to matter.

Twenty women in the office is a real advantage most accounts don't have. **Use it every single post.**

## How to run it

1. **The moment a post goes live, do a floor round.** Show the post, ask what they genuinely think or what they'd want to ask about the topic.
2. **Help them comment — in their words, from their account.** If someone's happy for you to type it on her phone, fine — but it's *her* question, phrased how she'd say it. With her okay, always.
3. **Real questions beat compliments.** "Does this apply to gold jewellery too?" starts a thread. "Great post 👏" doesn't.
4. **Then reply to every one of them from IFM** — that doubles the comment count and turns the post into a visible conversation.
5. **Rotate.** Different 6–8 women each post, not the same three every time.

## ✅ DO
- **Do this within the first hour.** That's the whole point — the same comments three hours later are worth half.
- **Ask for their real reaction** — the question a colleague actually has is the question a stranger has too.
- **Reply from IFM to every comment**, warmly, with a question back where it fits.
- **Rotate through everyone** so no account comments on every single post.

## ❌ DON'T
- **Never post from someone's phone without asking that time.** A standing "sure, whenever" from a colleague is still an ask-each-time.
- **Don't script identical comments.** Ten variations of the same sentence are obvious to anyone reading — and to Instagram.
- **Don't let it slip to "when I get a moment."** An afternoon round on a morning post is a missed post.

# BLOCK 3 · Replies, DMs & Enquiries — 25 min

## What this is

Every comment answered. Every DM answered. Every enquiry logged.

## Why it matters

Right now this is roughly **one comment a day** — genuinely small. It will grow, and the habit needs to exist before the volume arrives.

**Speed matters more than volume.** A reply within the hour continues a conversation. A reply the next day ends one.

## What a good reply looks like

The goal of every reply is **a second message**, not a polite full stop.

> ❌ *"Thank you so much 🙏"*
> ✅ *"Thank you! Curious — is it the jargon that puts you off, or not knowing where to start? We hear both a lot."*

Answer, then ask something back. Every time.

## When someone asks the price

This one matters, so here's exactly how to handle it. **Answer plainly.** Don't dodge it, don't disappear into DMs without answering, don't pounce.

> ✅ *"It's ₹[X] for the [N]-week batch, and the next one starts [date]. Happy to send you the full outline if you'd like — want me to?"*

Price, date, next step. Three things, no drama. **Then log it as an enquiry straight away.**

Dodging a price question loses the person. So does hard-selling them.

## ✅ DO

- **Answer within the hour** wherever you can.
- **Answer the actual question**, not the one you'd prefer they'd asked.
- **Log every enquiry the moment it happens** — name, contact, what they asked, where.
- **Always fill in the follow-up date — even when it's months away.** *"It's for my daughter, she starts in six months"* is a real lead, not a no. Put June 2027 in the column and it will come back to us. Without a date it simply disappears, and that's how most of our funnel has been lost.
- **Treat basic questions as the best questions.** Someone asking "what even is a SIP?" is exactly who IFM exists for.

## ❌ DON'T

- **Never let "Thanks 🙏" be an entire reply.**
- **Never skip a question because it seems too basic.**
- **Never hard-sell in a comment reply.** Answer first. The ask comes later, in DMs, if it fits naturally.
- **Don't let a DM sit overnight.**
- **Don't log enquiries at the end of the week.** You'll forget half and invent the rest.

# CLASS DAYS · Filming

**Every class gets filmed. No exceptions.** This is one of the highest-value things you'll do, and it's worth understanding why.

## Here's the evidence

| Post | Comments it got |
|---|---|
| **Workshop photos — Streedhan session** | **10** |
| **Founder story** | **4** |
| RBI Monetary Policy explainer | 0 |
| Repo rate explainer | 0 |
| Gold / central banks explainer | 0 |

Real people in real rooms beat polished explainers, decisively. **Our most effortful content performs the worst.** A phone clip of a mum laughing at one of our games is worth more than a beautifully produced explainer — and it's free.

## What to shoot — aim for 5 or 6 short clips

- [ ] **Wide shot of the room** — shows it's real and it's full
- [ ] **Faces reacting** — the moment something clicks
- [ ] **Hands on phones** — people playing the games
- [ ] **Hiral mid-explanation** — 10–15 seconds, no setup
- [ ] **The messy bits** — laughing, arguing over an answer
- [ ] **One person saying one line** — *"I never knew that"* is gold

## ✅ DO
- **Vertical. Always.**
- **Say it out loud at the start:** *"We film for social — just tell me if you'd rather not be on camera."* Then respect it absolutely.
- **Shoot more than you need.** Ten clips, use three.
- **Capture the room's energy**, not just the slides.

## ❌ DON'T
- **Never film anyone who's opted out.** Not once, not in the background.
- **Don't only film the front of the room.** The slides aren't the story. The faces are.
- **Don't shoot horizontal.** It's unusable for Stories and Reels.
- **Don't try to make it look professional.** Real is the point — polished performs worse.

**Then, the same day:** upload → catalogue → cut the best moments into Stories within 48 hours, while it's still fresh.

### Send the best clips to the feed as well — not just Stories

**Stories reach people who already follow us. Reels reach people who don't.** That difference is the whole reason we film.

Right now almost all our class footage stops at Stories, which means our best proof only ever reaches the 358 people who've already found us. **Every time you film, pick the two or three strongest clips and flag them for the agency's feed calendar.** Film once, publish twice.

*(Sending the clip on is all you do — the agency builds the feed post. You don't make the reel.)*
