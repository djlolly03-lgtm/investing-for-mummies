Aath seedhiyanThe eight ladders · plumbing, not virtue

  1. 3 → 22

    Saat din ka hisaab

    Seven days of writing down every rupee usually finds ₹1,500 to ₹3,000 a month leaking — ₹18,000 to ₹36,000 a year, found without earning any more.

    Karo: Notes app or the back of a bill. Seven days only. Cut nothing yet — just read it once at the end.

  2. 12 → 30

    Pehli SIP — ₹500

    ₹500 a month for twenty years is ₹1,20,000 of your own money going in, ₹500 at a time. The habit is what you are really buying.

    Karo: One index fund, direct plan, ₹500, auto-debit dated two days after salary. Fifteen minutes, once.

  3. 17 → 36

    Aadhaar se mobile jodo

    This one costs about ₹50 and one morning, and it is the single most common reason a first attempt to invest fails.

    Karo: Go to an Aadhaar Seva Kendra with your Aadhaar and the phone number you actually use today. That is it.

  4. 25 → 49

    Bura Waqt Fund

    If the house spends ₹25,000 a month, three months is ₹75,000. That is what stops a hospital bill turning into card debt at 42 paise a rupee.

    Karo: Open a separate savings account, no card linked. Send ₹2,000 a month until it holds three months of kharcha.

  5. 41 → 62

    Apna health cover

    One serious hospital admission costs ₹3,00,000 to ₹5,00,000. A ₹10,00,000 family floater costs roughly ₹20,000 to ₹30,000 a year.

    Karo: Buy a family floater of at least ₹10,00,000 in your own name, separate from the office cover, while everyone is healthy.

  6. 54 → 71

    Term insurance

    For a healthy 30-year-old, ₹1,00,00,000 of cover often costs about ₹12,000 to ₹15,000 a year — roughly ₹1,000 a month. Nothing comes back, which is exactly why it is cheap.

    Karo: Take 10 to 15 times your yearly income. Term only, bought online, nothing bundled with it, every health question answered honestly.

  7. 68 → 85

    Direct plan

    Same fund, same manager, two prices. Direct typically keeps about ₹500 a year in your hands for every ₹1,00,000 you hold — every year, for as long as you hold it.

    Karo: In the app, choose the Direct version and the Growth option. Check the exit load before moving old money.

  8. 78 → 94

    Kuch nahi kiya

    Prices fell 30% — ₹5,00,000 showed as ₹3,50,000 — and you changed nothing for eighteen months. Your ₹5,000 kept buying every month while everyone else stopped.

    Karo: Decide today, in writing, while things are calm: when it falls, I do nothing for eighteen months. Show that page to your family.

Aath saanpThe eight snakes · a product, never a person

  1. 27 → 9

    “FD se better” scheme

    ₹50,000 a year for 15 years is ₹7,50,000 of your money, and it comes back as about ₹10,40,000 — roughly ₹2,50,000 less than the same money in an FD.

    Bachne ka tareeka: Ask one question before signing — policy kitne saal ki hai? If the answer is 15 or 20, it is not an FD.

    Yeh paisa kisko gaya: the bank branch and the agent, from a large first-year commission.

  2. 38 → 20

    Card ka minimum due

    A ₹50,000 bill left running on the card costs about ₹21,000 of interest in a year. The “minimum due” of ₹2,500 keeps the card alive and the karza growing.

    Bachne ka tareeka: Pay the full bill on the date. If you truly cannot, ask the bank to convert it to a normal loan — 14 paise a rupee beats 42.

    Yeh paisa kisko gaya: the card issuer. Interest on revolving balances is most of what a card earns.

  3. 46 → 28

    3% har mahine

    ₹2,00,000 into a scheme paying 3% a month. It paid on time for eight months, then stopped. Nothing came back.

    Bachne ka tareeka: Ask where the profit comes from. If the answer is new members, it is a chain system — walk away, and warn the neighbour too.

    Yeh paisa kisko gaya: the earlier members and the organiser, paid out of the newer members’ money.

  4. 57 → 40

    Sona girvi rakha

    ₹4,00,000 borrowed against the family gold at 20 paise a rupee is ₹80,000 of interest a year. Miss the payments and the chain is auctioned, not returned.

    Bachne ka tareeka: Save for known kharcha months ahead. Gold is the last door to open, never the first.

    Yeh paisa kisko gaya: the gold-loan NBFC or the local lender, and whoever buys the auctioned gold.

  5. 66 → 47

    F&O ka chakkar

    SEBI’s own study: almost 88 of every 100 people who traded F&O last year lost money. The average net loss was ₹1,17,000 per person, in one year.

    Bachne ka tareeka: That is trading, not investing. If you want the market, use a SIP and give it years.

    Yeh paisa kisko gaya: the broker and the exchange. Transaction costs alone took about ₹25,000 crore.

  6. 74 → 55

    SIP band kar di

    Prices fell, so you paused ₹5,000 a month for eight months. That is ₹40,000 that never went in — and it never went in cheap.

    Bachne ka tareeka: If money is tight, cut the SIP to ₹500. Cutting is fine. Stopping is what hurts.

    Yeh paisa kisko gaya: nobody. This one costs you and pays no one — which is why almost nobody warns you about it.

  7. 89 → 79

    Ek aur premium

    A call says one more premium unlocks your stuck money. ₹30,000 paid — and it turned out to be a brand new 15-year policy.

    Bachne ka tareeka: Ask for the old policy number and its maturity date, in writing, before paying anything.

    Yeh paisa kisko gaya: the agent, who earns a fresh first-year commission on a “revival”.

  8. 96 → 88

    “CBI bol raha hoon”

    A video call, a police backdrop, your Aadhaar “found in a parcel”, and then ₹8,000 as a release fee. Indians lost about ₹3,012 crore to this between 2022 and 2025.

    Bachne ka tareeka: Cut the call, tell one family member, dial 1930 within the hour. No officer ever asks for a transfer.

    Yeh paisa kisko gaya: organised scam centres — around 46% of these operations run out of Cambodia, Myanmar and Laos.
