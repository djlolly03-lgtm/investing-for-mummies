# What Indian Households Actually Believe, Do and Get Wrong About Money — 2026 Field Brief

*Research compiled Sept 2026. Statistics sourced where available; judgement calls and practitioner inferences are flagged as **[uncertain]** or **[judgement]**. Where sources disagree, ranges are given.*

---

## 0. The three facts that should shape the whole game design

1. **The problem is not awareness. It is conversion and trust.** SEBI's Investor Survey 2025 (90,000+ households) found **63% of Indian households are aware of at least one securities market product** — but only **9.5% actually participate** (~3.21 crore households). Urban 15%, rural 6%. ([SEBI survey coverage](https://www.policyedge.in/p/sebi-investor-survey-2025-mapping))
2. **~80% of households explicitly prefer capital preservation over higher returns** — and so do **79% of Gen Z households**. Risk aversion is not a generational problem that will age out. ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/indian-households-including-gen-z-remain-risk-averse-sebi-survey-125093001479_1.html))
3. **94% of households prefer Hindi or a regional language over English for financial content.** Any game shipped English-first is speaking to a minority. (SEBI Investor Survey 2025)

A fourth, uncomfortable one: **only 21% of the public can identify SEBI as the securities regulator, vs 73% for RBI.** "SEBI-registered" as a safety cue is nearly meaningless to a mass audience — it cannot be the punchline of a scam-detection lesson.

## 1. The 15 most damaging, most widespread money beliefs

Ranked by (prevalence × rupee damage), not by how wrong they are.

### 1. "Insurance is an investment. My LIC policy is my savings."
**Truth:** Bundled savings-insurance products (endowment, money-back, ULIP) return roughly **3–5.5% IRR** — below FD, below inflation-plus-tax in many years — while giving cover of typically 10x annual premium instead of the 10–15x annual *income* a family actually needs.
**Stat:** Life insurance funds absorbed **₹5.3 lakh crore, ~15% of all household financial savings in FY25** — more than mutual funds (13%). India's **life insurance protection gap is 87%**, and penetration *fell* to 2.7% of GDP in FY25 from 2.8%. One worked example of a 15-year endowment on a 30-year-old: **net IRR 3.11%**. ([IRDAI/BS](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/insurance/india-s-insurance-penetration-needle-remains-flat-at-3-7-in-2024-25-126010900679_1.html), [PrimeInvestor](https://primeinvestor.in/is-there-still-a-case-for-an-endowment-plan/))
**Why it's the #1 target:** it is the single largest voluntary misallocation in the Indian household — bigger than F&O losses, and it happens to people who think they are being *careful*.

### 2. "I bought a policy, so I'm covered" — but it lapses.
**Truth:** Most people cannot sustain a 15–20 year premium they were sold on a tax-season impulse. Lapsing early is worse than never buying: surrender values in the first few years are punitive or zero.
**Stat:** On a **policy-count basis, 13th-month persistency was ~64% and 61st-month ~50%** (LIC FY25 disclosure; industry-wide figures are similar or worse). Roughly **one in three policies dies before its first renewal; about half are gone by year five**. ([LIC press release, 27 May 2025](https://licindia.in/documents/d/guest/press-release-dtd-27052025-final))

### 3. "Property always goes up. Land never disappoints."
**Truth:** Indian residential real estate has delivered roughly **4–7% CAGR nationally over the last decade** (highly city-dependent), with **net rental yields of 2–3% — among the lowest in the world** — plus stamp duty, brokerage, maintenance, and near-zero liquidity. Nifty 50 TRI has done **~14.6% CAGR over 20 years**.
**Stat:** India's house price index rose **31.6% between 2017 and 2024 ≈ 4% CAGR**; Mumbai/Bengaluru/NCR net rental yields **2–3%**. ([99acres](https://www.99acres.com/articles/why-is-rental-yield-one-of-the-lowest-in-india.html), [Wright Research](https://www.wrightresearch.in/blog/investing-in-india-equities-vs-real-estate/))
**Consequence:** ~**49% of household gross wealth sits in property**, much of it a second flat bought as "investment" that yields 2% and can't be sold in a hurry.

### 4. "Gold is safe and always goes up."
**Truth:** Gold has had a spectacular run (gold price index **+144% March 2024 → March 2026**; over **₹1.5 lakh/10g in 2026**), which is precisely why the belief is currently unfalsifiable in conversation. Gold has also had 8-year flat stretches (2012–2019). It pays no income, costs 8–25% in making charges on jewellery, and jewellery is the worst possible form to hold it in.
**Stat:** Indian households hold **~25,000 tonnes** (World Gold Council) — Morgan Stanley puts total at **34,600 tonnes / $3.8 trillion ≈ 89% of India's GDP**; **only ~15% is monetised**. ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stocks/story/indians-own-38-trillion-worth-gold-wealth-effect-for-household-balance-sheet-497717-2025-10-10), [Business Today, Sept 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy/story/only-15-of-indias-gold-is-monetised-why-gold-loans-could-become-a-new-consumption-engine-552405-2026-09-02))
**Teach the nuance, not the negation:** the game should not say "gold is bad." It should say "jewellery ≠ investment, and 40% in gold is not diversification."

### 5. "FD is safe. My money can't lose value."
**Truth:** FD protects nominal rupees and loses purchasing power. At **CPI ~4.4% (July 2026)** and FD ~6.5%, a 30% taxpayer earns **~4.6% post-tax = ~0.2% real**. At 6% inflation the same FD is *negative*.
**Stat:** CPI 4.45% July 2026; **deposits = 35% of household financial savings flows (₹12.5 lakh crore) in FY25**, the single largest bucket. ([RBI via IndMoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/stocks/indian-household-savings-allocation-fy25))
**The lesson that lands:** "safe" means *nothing can go wrong*, not *nothing changes*. Mehngai is the thing going wrong, silently.

### 6. "The stock market is a satta bazaar — you either get lucky or get wiped out."
**Truth:** This belief is *self-fulfilling*, because the people who act on the excitement version of it go straight to F&O and intraday, prove the belief, and tell everyone.
**Stat:** **87.7% of individual equity-derivatives traders lost money in FY26**, aggregate net loss **₹91,685 crore**; average net loss **₹1.17 lakh per trader**; **options accounted for ~92% of losses**; transaction costs alone ate **~₹25,000 crore**. Among under-30s (43% of all traders), **89% lost**. **~90% of those who lost two years running and kept trading lost again.** ([SEBI study, 20 Aug 2026](https://openthemagazine.com/business/sebi-fo-loss-study-explained-why-9-in-10-retail-traders-lost-91685-crore-in-fy26), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/active-derivatives-traders-fall-18-in-fy26-to-8-75-million-sebi-study-126082001378_1.html))
**Prior years:** 93% loss-makers FY22–24, ₹1.8 lakh crore aggregate; ~91% and ~₹1.05–1.12 lakh crore in FY25 **[sources differ on the FY25 total]**. Intraday cash: **71% loss-makers FY23, 76% among young traders**, average loss ₹5,371.

### 7. "Trading and investing are the same thing."
**Truth:** They are opposite activities with opposite time horizons, opposite skill requirements and opposite base rates. The word "market" covers both, and that ambiguity is where households are destroyed.
**Stat:** 23.44 crore demat accounts (July 2026) vs **6.14 crore unique mutual fund investors by PAN (March 2026)** — many people's first market experience is a broking app, not a fund. ([AMFI](https://www.amfiindia.com/uploads/AMFI_Monthly_Note_Mar_2026_1f0a1f5c48.pdf), [Asianet](https://newsable.asianetnews.com/business/demat-account-additions-surge-in-july-2026-reach-234-4-million-articleshow-hykhvbp))
**[Judgement]** This is arguably the highest-leverage single distinction in the entire curriculum.

### 8. "If someone on YouTube/Telegram is confident and has followers, they know."
**Truth:** Confidence and reach are free; a SEBI RIA licence is not.
**Stat:** **62% of prospective investors are influenced by finfluencers** (SEBI Investor Survey 2025). SEBI has taken down **70,000+ misleading posts/accounts since Oct 2024**, barred registered intermediaries from associating with unregistered finfluencers (circular 29 Jan 2025), and in Dec 2025 **impounded ₹546 crore** from one "trading academy" running unregistered advisory under an education label. ([SEBI/Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/securities/1726258/sebis-crackdown-on-finfluencers-regulations-and-enforcement))

### 9. "Guaranteed high return" is a category that exists.
**Truth:** In India, guaranteed nominal returns above ~7.5% are either (a) mislabelled insurance, or (b) illegal. Above ~12%, it is a Ponzi with near-certainty.
**Stat:** The Unregulated Deposit Schemes Act 2019 explicitly bans such schemes; the pattern repeats at scale — Saradha (~₹30,000 crore, 17 lakh depositors), PACL (₹49,100 crore), Rose Valley (₹15,000+ crore), and in Dec 2025 an ED action on a **₹2,300 crore crypto Ponzi/MLM**. ([ED via BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/india-crypto-ponzi-bust-global-scam-losses/))
**Design note:** the word "guaranteed" is the most *dangerously well-understood* English financial word in India. It is the scammer's keyword and everyone knows it.

### 10. "Health emergencies are handled by the family / by savings / by loans."
**Truth:** One hospitalisation is the most common route from lower-middle class to debt in India.
**Stat:** **~40 crore Indians — the "missing middle," ~30% of the population — have no health insurance**; out-of-pocket spend is **roughly 40–50% of total health expenditure [sources range 39–62%; uncertain]**; **17% of households spend more than 10% of income on health**. ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/opinion/indias-400-million-strong-missing-middle-uninsured-and-one-illness-away-from-financial-ruin/))
**Linked belief:** "my company gives health cover, so I'm fine" — true until the day the job ends, which is the day cover ends and pre-existing conditions reset.

### 11. "Borrow for the wedding / the phone / the festival — everyone does."
**Truth:** Household leverage is now rising faster than household assets, and the borrowing is increasingly *consumption*, not asset creation.
**Stat:** **Household debt = 41.3% of GDP at March 2025** (RBI FSR), up from ~33% in FY19; RBI FSR June 2026 puts it at **45.5% [single-source, treat as indicative]**. **Household financial liabilities grew 102% between FY20 and FY25 vs 48% growth in assets.** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/indias-household-debt-rises-to-41-3-per-cent-of-gdp-rbi-fsr-125123101081_1.html))

### 12. "Minimum due on the credit card is the bill."
**Truth:** Card revolve rates run 36–48% p.a. Paying the minimum is the most expensive borrowing an ordinary household ever does, and it's invisible because no one ever quotes the annual rate.
**Stat:** Credit card outstanding has crossed **₹3 lakh crore**; **credit card NPAs jumped ~28% in FY25**; BNPL disbursed **~₹97,000 crore in H1 FY25**, and **68% of first-ever CIBIL score declines in the 18–25 cohort were attributed to missed BNPL payments [secondary source, uncertain]**. ([Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/loan-defaults-in-india-rise-to-over-3-percent-in-fy25-credit-card-npas-jump-28-percent), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/credit-boom-shows-cracks-rising-defaults-in-cards-personal-auto-loans-125081200655_1.html))

### 13. "Gold loan is cheap and easy — it's my own gold."
**Truth:** It is now the fastest-growing form of household leverage in India, increasingly used for consumption rather than emergencies, and default means losing the family's only real reserve asset.
**Stat:** Outstanding gold loans went from **₹6.3 lakh crore (Mar 2023) to ₹19.4 lakh crore (Mar 2026)** — roughly tripling; bank loans against gold jewellery **+105.5% YoY to ~₹5.1 lakh crore (May 2026)**. **[The ₹19 lakh crore figure spans banks + NBFCs + informal and is not directly comparable to the bank-only number.]** ([Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/market-news/gold-loans-rise-50-percent-india-fy26/), [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/news/india-consumption-loans-q1fy26-gold-loans-crif-report-125090201207_1.html))

### 14. "I'll start investing when I have a lump sum / after this expense / next year."
**Truth:** Time in market beats amount. A 25-year-old investing ₹2,000/month beats a 35-year-old investing ₹5,000/month, and almost nobody believes this until they see the number.
**Stat:** ~10.6 crore SIP accounts, ₹32,000 crore/month, average retail folio ticket **₹86,877** — the median Indian investor is investing small amounts, so the *starting age* is the whole game. And **~40% of SEBI-surveyed investors are dormant**, 87% of them citing poor performance — i.e. they started, saw a drawdown, and froze.

### 15. "Nominee / will / paperwork — I'll do it later."
**Truth:** Money that nobody can find is money that was never earned.
**Stat:** As of Feb 2026: **₹60,518 crore unclaimed with public sector banks** in RBI's DEA Fund, **₹8,974 crore unclaimed with life insurers**, **₹3,749 crore unclaimed in mutual funds**. A national campaign returned only **₹5,777 crore across 22.95 lakh claims**. ([PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2244475&reg=48&lang=2))

**Honourable mentions (16–20), for later modules:** "tax-saving = investing" (the March ELSS/insurance panic buy); "direct plan and regular plan are the same, my agent's advice is free"; "SIP means guaranteed positive returns"; "the market falling means I should stop my SIP" (the SIP stoppage ratio spikes precisely during drawdowns — it hit ~109% in Jan 2025); "retirement will be handled by my children."

## 2. Where household money actually sits

### A. The stock (balance sheet — what families *own*)
**[Approximate; drawn from UBS Global Wealth Report / Morgan Stanley / Jefferies estimates — directionally reliable, precise splits uncertain]**

| Asset | Share of gross household wealth |
|---|---|
| Real estate (primary home, plots, second property) | **~49%** |
| Gold (and silver) | **~15%** |
| **All financial assets combined** | **~26%** |
| Other physical (vehicles, machinery, livestock, business assets) | ~10% |

Within that ~26% of financial assets, **bank deposits are roughly half** (~54%); direct equity + mutual funds together are a small minority. India's financial-asset share is **among the lowest in the world** for a country of its size and growth rate.

Gold alone is **~89% of India's GDP** in value (Morgan Stanley, Oct 2025) and Indian households hold **more gold than the top 10 central banks combined**.

### B. The flow (RBI FY25 — where *this year's savings* went)

Gross household financial savings: **11.8% of GNDI** (down from 12.1%); net: **7.0% of GNDI** (up from 5.8%).

| Instrument | ₹ lakh crore | Share of financial savings |
|---|---|---|
| Bank & non-bank deposits | 12.5 | **35%** |
| Provident & pension funds | 7.9 | **22%** |
| **Life insurance funds** | **5.3** | **15%** |
| Mutual funds | 4.7 | **13%** |
| Small savings (PO, NSC, KVP) | 2.3 | 7% |
| Currency (cash) | 2.1 | 6% |
| **Direct equity** | **0.7** | **2%** |

Separately, **savings in physical assets ran at ~12.9% of GDP**, with **gold & silver ornaments at ~0.2% of GDP (~₹65,000 crore/year)** — note the flow into gold is small; the *stock* is enormous and inherited.

**The one-line summary for the game:** *79 paise of every 100 rupees a household saves goes into deposits, PF, insurance and post office. 13 paise goes to mutual funds. 2 paise goes to shares. And behind all of it sits a house and a locker full of gold that together are two-thirds of the family's net worth.*

### C. Participation counters (mid-2026)
- Demat accounts: **23.44 crore** (CDSL ~18.8 cr, NSDL ~4.6 cr) — inflated by duplicates
- Unique MF investors (by PAN): **6.14 crore** (Mar 2026), up from 5.36 crore a year earlier
- MF folios: **28.09 crore**; MF AUM **₹85.76 lakh crore** (Jul 2026)
- SIP accounts: **~10.63 crore**; monthly SIP flow **~₹32,000 crore**
- B30 (beyond top-30 cities) = **18.9% of MF assets**, average ticket **₹93,000** vs T30's **₹2.45 lakh**
- EPFO active members ~**8 crore**; NPS **2.12 crore** subscribers (₹16.1 lakh cr AUM); APY **8.96 crore** enrolments — yet **only ~12% of the workforce has any formal pension**
- Households actually investing in securities: **9.5%**

## 3. The mis-selling traps — product, seller, and the pitch line

**[The pitch lines below are reconstructions of the standard, widely-reported scripts, not verbatim survey quotes. They are what practitioners and consumer-forum complaints consistently describe. Use them as game dialogue; don't cite them as data.]**

Regulatory backdrop: IRDAI's own annual report calls mis-selling a **"significant concern."** Grievances filed under *unfair business practices* rose **14% to 26,667 in FY25**, now **22.14% of all complaints against life insurers**. **Banks and brokers generate more mis-selling complaints than any other channel.** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/insurance/mis-selling-indian-insurance-sector-significant-concern-irdai-125123001092_1.html))

### Trap 1 — The bank branch "better than FD" pitch
**Product:** Single-premium or 5-pay endowment / guaranteed-return insurance plan
**Sold by:** Bank relationship manager, at the moment your FD matures or a large credit lands
**Pitch lines:**
- *"Sir, FD mein toh 6.5% hai aur uspe tax bhi lagega. Yeh humari nayi scheme hai — guaranteed return, aur tax-free."*
- *"Sirf 5 saal bharna hai, uske baad paisa aapka."*
- *"Yeh FD ka hi upgraded version hai, bas insurance bhi free mein mil raha hai."*
**What's actually happening:** A 15–20 year contract with 4–5% IRR, front-loaded commissions, brutal surrender penalties, sold as a 5-year deposit. The "5 saal" is the *premium-paying* term, not the *policy* term.
**Tell:** the words "5 saal bharna hai" — always ask "policy kitne saal ki hai?"

### Trap 2 — The loan bundling trap
**Product:** Life/property/credit insurance attached to a home or personal loan
**Sold by:** Loan officer / DSA at sanction stage
**Pitch line:** *"Insurance lena compulsory hai loan ke liye — file aage nahi badhegi."*
**Truth:** Bundling insurance as a *precondition* is not permitted; the premium is often financed into the loan so you pay interest on it for 20 years.

### Trap 3 — The March tax-panic sale
**Product:** ELSS sold badly, or (worse) endowment/ULIP sold as 80C
**Sold by:** Agent, CA's assistant, office "insurance guy," WhatsApp
**Pitch lines:**
- *"31 March se pehle 1.5 lakh nahi lagaya toh tax kat jayega."*
- *"Tax bhi bachega aur return bhi milega."*
**Truth:** A 15-year commitment made in a 3-day panic, chosen for a deduction worth ₹46,800, is how most households acquire their worst product. Under the new tax regime this pitch is often simply irrelevant to the buyer — and still gets made.

### Trap 4 — The ULIP-vs-mutual-fund switch
**Product:** ULIP
**Sold by:** Bancassurance and agency channel
**Pitch lines:**
- *"Mutual fund mein LTCG tax lagta hai, ULIP mein nahi."*
- *"Isme bhi market ka return milega, aur insurance bhi free."*
- *"Paisa double ho jayega 7-8 saal mein."*
**Truth:** Mortality charges, premium allocation charges, policy admin charges and fund management charges stack up; 5-year lock-in; switching is not free in the ways implied; the "free" insurance is paid for out of your units every single month.

### Trap 5 — The "regular plan is free" omission
**Product:** Regular-plan mutual funds
**Sold by:** Distributor / bank / app
**Pitch line:** *"Mera service charge kuch nahi hai, sab free hai."*
**Truth:** ~0.6–1.2% p.a. trail commission, embedded in NAV, compounding against the investor for 20 years. **[Distributors handle ~72% of individual assets — AMFI Jan 2026.]**

### Trap 6 — The NFO "₹10 par unit is cheap" pitch
**Product:** New Fund Offers, especially thematic/sectoral
**Pitch line:** *"Naya fund hai, sirf ₹10 ka NAV — abhi lo, sasta hai."*
**Truth:** NAV level is meaningless. A fresh NFO is a *worse*-informed purchase than a fund with a 10-year record.

### Trap 7 — The relationship-churn
**Product:** Whatever pays this quarter
**Sold by:** RMs with quarterly targets; the RM changes every 12–18 months
**Pitch line:** *"Sir, ab yeh purana ho gaya, isko switch kar dete hain naye scheme mein."*
**Truth:** Every switch is a fresh commission and often a fresh exit load / lock-in.

### Trap 8 — The "revive your lapsed policy" call
**Product:** Revival of a dead endowment policy, or a "loan promise" pitch
**Pitch line:** *"Aapka paisa fasa hua hai, ek aur premium bhar do toh sab wapas mil jayega."*
**Truth:** Frequently a fresh policy sold as a revival. This is a documented recurring pattern in insurance-ombudsman complaints.

## 4. The scams currently hitting Indian households

**Scale:** Indians lost **~₹22,495 crore to cyber fraud in 2025, up 24% YoY**. In **March 2026 alone, 524,121 suspected mule accounts/UPI VPAs were flagged**. Authorities deactivated 1.2 million SIMs and froze 1.33 million mule accounts in 2025, recovering ₹5,489 crore. ([InsightsOnIndia](https://www.insightsonindia.com/2026/02/21/cybercrime-in-india/), [The420](https://the420.in/india-mule-accounts-upi-fraud-march-2026-report/))

| Scam | The hook, in one line |
|---|---|
| **Fake trading app** | A polished app that shows your money growing beautifully — until you try to withdraw and are told to pay "tax" first. |
| **WhatsApp/Telegram stock group** | You're added to a "VIP" group full of strangers posting profit screenshots and thanking sir; the tips work for two weeks, then the big one arrives. |
| **Digital arrest** | A "CBI/customs/TRAI officer" video-calls from a fake police-station backdrop, says a parcel with drugs has your Aadhaar on it, and keeps you on camera until you transfer everything "for verification." |
| **Guaranteed-return / Ponzi** | A neighbour or community elder shows you *his* returns — 3% a month, paid on time, for eight months — and offers to introduce you. |
| **Illegal loan app** | ₹5,000 in 5 minutes with no paperwork; the app quietly takes your contacts and photos, and the "recovery" starts as threats to your entire address book. |
| **Task/part-time job scam** | "Like these YouTube videos, earn ₹150" — you're paid twice, then asked to deposit into a "merchant task" to unlock bigger payouts. |
| **Fake IPO / pre-IPO allotment** | "Unlisted shares of a company about to list — guaranteed allotment, 3x on listing day." |
| **Opinion-trading / prediction apps** | Betting dressed in market vocabulary — "profit," "stop loss," "trading" — advertised as a game of skill. |
| **Crypto MLM** | A "mining pool" or token with a referral tree; the returns are real until the recruitment stops. |
| **KYC-expiry / SIM-block** | "Your bank KYC expires today, click this link" — the link is a screen-sharing app (AnyDesk/TeamViewer). |
| **Fake customer-care number** | You Google the bank's helpline, call the sponsored fake, and are walked into installing remote access. |
| **UPI "wrong transfer" refund** | "Bhai, galti se aapke account mein ₹9,000 chala gaya, wapas kar do" — you approve a *collect request* instead of sending. |
| **Deepfake endorsement** | A video of a famous investor or news anchor personally recommending a "government-backed AI trading platform." |
| **Fake insurance/policy-recovery** | "IRDAI has recovered your lapsed policy money — pay processing fee to release it." Preys on the ₹8,974 crore of genuinely unclaimed insurance money. |

**Structural facts worth putting in the game:**
- Digital arrest: **241,537 cases and ~₹3,012 crore lost 2022–2025**; **>30,000 complaints in 2025**; **~46% of these operations are run out of Cambodia, Myanmar and Laos** (I4C).
- Loan apps: RBI's **Digital Lending Directions 2025** + a **public directory of legitimate DLAs on rbi.org.in since 1 July 2025**; RBI had Google remove **47 apps** in 2026; MeitY earlier banned **94 Chinese-linked loan apps** with effective rates up to 3,000% annualised.
- **One universal defence line for the game:** *no genuine police, court, CBI, RBI or income-tax officer has ever asked anyone to transfer money to a "verification account."* Helpline **1930**; portal cybercrime.gov.in; the **golden hour** (report within 60 minutes) is when money can still be frozen.

## 5. What a first-time investor actually has to DO — and exactly where they get stuck

### The real-world sequence

**Step 0 — Prerequisites (this is where most journeys die)**
1. **PAN card** — and the name on it must match Aadhaar *exactly*.
2. **Aadhaar with a live linked mobile number.** ← *Biggest single failure point.*
3. **A bank account in the same name**, with net banking or a cancelled cheque.
4. **Mobile number and email registered on that bank account.**

**Step 1 — KYC (once, industry-wide)**
- Aadhaar OTP e-KYC → PAN verification → photo/signature upload → **In-Person Verification via a short live or recorded video** → Aadhaar e-Sign.
- Takes 20–30 minutes if documents are clean; account activation typically 24–48 hours.

**Step 2 — Choose the road**
- **Mutual funds only:** no demat needed. Any AMC site, MF Central, or an RTA (CAMS/KFintech). Simplest path, and the correct one for ~95% of first-timers.
- **Stocks/ETFs:** need a **demat + trading account** with a broker (this bundles the depository account with CDSL/NSDL).

**Step 3 — Add a nominee.** Now mandatory in practice and skipped by almost everyone.

**Step 4 — Set the SIP mandate**
- e-NACH / UPI AutoPay mandate authorising monthly auto-debit. Requires bank OTP or debit-card authentication. Set the SIP date **2–3 days after salary credit.**

**Step 5 — Fund and buy.** Choose *Direct* plan, *Growth* option. First unit allotment on the next business day at that day's NAV.

**Step 6 — Keep the plumbing alive.** Re-KYC when address changes; keep the CAS (consolidated account statement) email; tell one family member the account exists.

### Where they actually get stuck — ranked

1. **Aadhaar mobile mismatch.** The OTP goes to a number they lost in 2018. Fix requires a physical Aadhaar Seva Kendra visit. **This kills more onboardings than any other single cause.** [Judgement, but consistently reported]
2. **Name mismatch across PAN / Aadhaar / bank.** "Rajesh Kumar Sharma" vs "Rajesh K Sharma" vs a maiden name on the bank account. Silent rejection days later, no clear reason given.
3. **Signature upload.** A photo of a signature on white paper, "clear, no shadow" — genuinely hard on a shared phone.
4. **The video IPV.** Rejection for lighting, network, or holding the PAN card the wrong way round, with a generic failure message.
5. **The mandate step.** Two OTPs, a bank redirect, a session timeout. If it fails, the SIP silently just never runs — and the investor thinks they've invested.
6. **The risk-profiling questionnaire.** Six English questions about "risk tolerance" and "investment horizon" that mean nothing, answered randomly.
7. **Choosing a fund.** 1,500+ schemes; no non-commercial default. This is where the app's promoted fund, or the neighbour's tip, wins.
8. **The first drawdown.** The portfolio shows −8% in week three. Roughly **40% of surveyed investors go dormant**, 87% citing poor performance. Nobody prepared them for the number turning red.
9. **Nominee skipped.** The screen says "skip for now," so they skip.
10. **Regular vs Direct.** Never explained, never noticed, costs ~1% a year forever.

**Game design implication:** the highest-value thing an educational game can do is not teach compounding — it is walk a player through *Aadhaar-mobile → PAN name match → mandate → nominee* as a five-minute simulated obstacle course, so the real-world attempt succeeds on the first try.

## 6. The 10 concepts ranked by impact-per-minute-of-teaching

Ranked by (damage prevented + benefit created) ÷ (minutes to genuinely land). This is the recommended curriculum spine.

| # | Concept | Teach time | Why it's this high |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Insurance and investment are two different things — never buy them in one product** | **~3 min** | Highest damage-per-minute in Indian finance. Reallocates 15% of national household savings flow. Reframes the single largest voluntary misallocation. One sentence, once understood, is permanent. |
| **2** | **"Guaranteed high return" = fraud. Above ~8% guaranteed, walk away** | **~2 min** | Cheapest defence line in existence. Blocks Ponzi, fake apps, Telegram groups, crypto MLM, pre-IPO scams, all with one heuristic. |
| **3** | **Nobody legitimate asks you to transfer money to "verify" it** | **~2 min** | One line defeats digital arrest, KYC-expiry, refund scams, and courier scams. ₹22,495 crore/year of national loss sits behind this sentence. |
| **4** | **Inflation: your "safe" money is shrinking** | **~4 min** | The gateway concept. Without it, "why leave the FD at all?" has no answer, and every subsequent lesson fails. Best taught as a price memory: what a movie ticket / kilo of dal / litre of petrol cost in 2006. |
| **5** | **Compounding, felt — not the formula** | **~5 min** | Highest upside-creation. Must be taught as *time*, not rate: "start at 25 vs 35" with rupee numbers, not a chart. |
| **6** | **Emergency fund before any investment: 6 months of expenses, liquid** | **~3 min** | The thing that stops the gold loan, the credit-card revolve, and the panic SIP-stop. Also the only reason SIPs survive a market fall. |
| **7** | **Trading ≠ investing, and 88% of traders lose** | **~4 min** | One SEBI number ends the debate. Prevents the single largest fast-loss channel for young men. Highest urgency in the 18–30 cohort. |
| **8** | **Health cover for the family, separate from employer cover** | **~4 min** | Prevents the most common single-event route to household bankruptcy — 40 crore people exposed. |
| **9** | **Cost matters: Direct vs Regular, expense ratio, commission** | **~5 min** | Silent, permanent 0.6–1.2%/year leak on every rupee. Lower urgency than 1–8, but compounds for 30 years. |
| **10** | **Nominee, paperwork, and telling your family where the money is** | **~3 min** | ₹73,000+ crore of unclaimed money says this is real. Low glamour, high certainty of payoff, and emotionally powerful in a family game format. |

**Deliberately below the line** (real, but poor impact-per-minute for a mass audience): asset allocation and rebalancing, tax-loss harvesting, NAV mechanics, P/E ratios, index vs active, debt-fund duration, LTCG/STCG detail, XIRR. These matter to the 9.5% who already invest, not to the 90% who don't.

## 7. Language reality — the 25-term table

### The evidence
- **94% of households prefer Hindi or a regional language over English** for financial information (SEBI Investor Survey 2025).
- **Only 36% of *actual investors*** have "high or moderate" knowledge of the products they own.
- **<1% have attended any formal investor-education programme**, though **91% of those who did found it useful.**
- Barriers cited by non-investors: **product complexity / information gaps 74%**, risk-return concerns 73%, trust 51%.

### The three tiers of comprehension
**[This classification is practitioner judgement, informed by AMFI/mutual-fund campaign practice and the SEBI language finding — it is not survey-verified term-by-term.]**

- **Tier A — English word already universal**, even in tier-2/3: EMI, loan, KYC, PAN, Aadhaar, CIBIL, ATM, UPI, balance, cash, bank, policy, premium, agent, nominee, guarantee, market, tax, budget, credit card, interest (as a word), branch, form.
- **Tier B — Word recognised as a *brand*, meaning not understood**: mutual fund, SIP, demat, NAV, ULIP, IPO, share market. People will say the word confidently and cannot define it. This is the most dangerous tier — it produces *false* confidence, and it is exactly where mis-selling lives.
- **Tier C — Not understood at all**: diversification, liquidity, portfolio, asset allocation, expense ratio, volatility, capital gains, corpus, rebalancing, equity, net worth, compounding (as a concept, though *chakravriddhi* is a recognised school word).

**The critical design rule:** shuddh Hindi textbook translations (*nivesh, jokhim, aapatkalin nidhi, prativarsh pratifal*) are recognised but **not felt** — they read as government pamphlet and produce nodding without understanding. The register that works is **spoken Hinglish**: English nouns people already own, wrapped in Hindi verbs and Hindi *consequences*.

### The table

| # | English term | Understood as English? | What to actually say (Hinglish) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Investment | Partly | **"paisa lagana"** | *Nivesh* is bookish. "Kahan paisa laga rahe ho?" is how people really ask. |
| 2 | Savings | Yes | **"bachat"** | Both work. |
| 3 | Interest | Yes | **"byaj"** | Universal. |
| 4 | Compound interest | No | **"byaj pe byaj"** | *Chakravriddhi byaj* is recognised from school but carries no feeling. "Byaj pe byaj" lands instantly. |
| 5 | Inflation | **No** | **"mehngai"** | Non-negotiable. Never write "inflation" in a mass-market game. |
| 6 | Returns | Word yes, % no | **"kitna badhkar mila"** | People think in *amounts*, not percentages. Always show rupees. |
| 7 | Risk | Yes (the word) | **"paisa doob sakta hai"** | *Jokhim* is formal. The felt word is **doobna**. |
| 8 | Diversification | **No** | **"paisa alag-alag jagah rakho"** / *"sare ande ek tokri mein mat rakho"* | The egg-basket proverb genuinely travels. |
| 9 | Mutual fund | Brand-recognised, not understood | **"sabka paisa ek jagah jama, expert manager chalata hai"** | AMFI's *"Mutual Funds Sahi Hai"* made the phrase famous without making it clear. |
| 10 | SIP | Brand-recognised | **"har mahine thoda-thoda"** | Best-penetrated financial acronym in India after EMI. |
| 11 | Equity / shares | "Share" yes, "equity" no | **"share"** / **"company mein hissa"** | Say *hissedari*, never *equity*. |
| 12 | Stock market | Yes | **"share bazaar"** | Universal, including the negative connotation of *satta bazaar*. |
| 13 | Volatility | No | **"utaar-chadhav"** | Rare case where the Hindi is better and already universal. |
| 14 | Liquidity | **No** | **"zaroorat pade toh kitni jaldi paisa nikal sakte ho"** | Frame as speed of access, never as a noun. |
| 15 | Lock-in | No | **"kitne saal paisa nahi nikal sakte"** | The most under-disclosed feature of mis-sold products. |
| 16 | Emergency fund | **No** | **"bura waqt fund"** / "3–6 mahine ka kharcha alag rakho" | *Aapatkalin nidhi* is pure pamphlet. |
| 17 | Term insurance | Partly | **"sirf suraksha wali policy — paisa wapas nahi milta, par ghar walon ko bada amount milta hai"** | Must include "paisa wapas nahi milta" up front or it feels like a con. |
| 18 | Premium | Yes | **"premium"** / "policy ki kist" | Fine as-is. |
| 19 | Sum assured | No | **"claim mein kitna milega"** | *Bima rashi* is formal but acceptable in print. |
| 20 | Maturity | Yes | **"maturity pe kitna milega"** | Understood. |
| 21 | Nominee | Yes | **"nominee"** | One of the best-penetrated English words — leverage it. |
| 22 | Expense ratio / commission | **No** | **"company har saal kitna kaat leti hai"** | Frame as an annual *cut*, in rupees per lakh. |
| 23 | Capital gains tax | No | **"munafe pe tax"** | |
| 24 | Credit score | "CIBIL" yes | **"CIBIL score"** | Say CIBIL, not "credit score." |
| 25 | Portfolio / net worth | **No** | **"aapka paisa kahan-kahan laga hai"** / **"kul jama-poonji"** | Never use "portfolio" in a mass-market screen. |

**Bonus terms worth pre-deciding:**
- **"Guaranteed"** — universally understood *and* the #1 scam trigger word. Treat it in the game as a red flag, not a feature.
- **"Girvi rakhna"** (pledging) carries the shame and the stakes that "gold loan" launders away — use it deliberately when teaching leverage.
- **"Chain system"** is the existing folk mental model for a Ponzi/MLM. Build on it rather than importing "Ponzi."
- **"Paisa double"** is the pitch line of nearly every scam and mis-sale in India. It should be the game's recurring villain phrase.

## Confidence notes

- **High confidence:** SEBI Investor Survey 2025 figures, SEBI F&O studies (FY24/25/26), AMFI folio/SIP/AUM data, IRDAI penetration and grievance counts, RBI household financial savings flow composition, unclaimed-asset totals (PIB, Feb 2026).
- **Medium confidence:** household balance-sheet split (49% property / 15% gold / 26% financial) — sourced from UBS/Morgan Stanley/Jefferies estimates that differ in methodology; household debt-to-GDP for FY26 (45.5%) rests on a single secondary source; India's gold tonnage (25,000 vs 34,600 tonnes depends on whether temple gold and revaluation are included).
- **Low confidence / flagged:** credit-card and BNPL delinquency percentages (secondary aggregators, definitions inconsistent); out-of-pocket health expenditure share (sources range 39%–62%); opinion-trading market size; the FY25 F&O aggregate loss (₹1.05 lakh cr vs ₹1.12 lakh cr across sources).
- **Dated:** the "27% of Indian adults are financially literate" figure is from the **NCFE 2019** survey. No newer national NCFE measurement was found. Use with the year attached, or prefer the SEBI 2025 knowledge figures instead.
- **Judgement, not data:** the Tier A/B/C language classification, the mis-selling pitch lines, and the onboarding failure-point ranking. These are practitioner reconstructions — strong enough for game design, not strong enough to cite as research findings.
