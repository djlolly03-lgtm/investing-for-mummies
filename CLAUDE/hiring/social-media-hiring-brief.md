## 0. Why we are hiring (read this before writing any job ad)

Two failures, and they are distinct:

1. **Almost nobody sees us.** 344 followers, growing 1.47/day. The people who do see IFM engage at a better-than-average rate — there are just very few of them. Meanwhile 38 finished, paid-for assets sit unpublished while lifetime posts total 46. The constraint is distribution, not content quality and not engagement quality.
2. **The courses have no ask.** Zero of the 12 audited captions contain a call to action, a price, or a date. The three that mention a workshop are all retrospective recaps. This isn't under-pushing; there is no ask at all.

**Do not merge these into one "social media manager" role.** Role 1 is a daily habit executed by a doer. Role 2 is a 6–8 week build by a specialist. Hiring one person for both reliably produces someone who just makes more posts — and we already have 38 posts made and unpublished.

**Before either hire, do the free thing:** put one line of CTA at the end of every caption, starting with the next post. It costs nothing and it is the largest single gap the audit found.

## The one-line version
You are not here to make content — we already have more than we publish. You are here to get it seen by people who aren't following us yet, and to make sure everyone who does react gets a reply.

## What you'll actually do

**First 6 weeks — clear the backlog.** 38 finished assets are sitting unpublished, including 11 game reels and 10 Wealth Conversation videos. Getting them out on a schedule is priority one and roughly doubles the account's lifetime output.

**Daily (approx. 4 hrs), in priority order**
1. **Outbound (90 min).** Comment as IFM on 20–30 posts from adjacent creators, mum communities, and parenting/personal-finance accounts — and participate genuinely in Facebook mum groups. Not "great post 🔥" — actual contributions. This is the growth engine and the largest block of your day.
2. **Publish (45 min).** Ship from the backlog on schedule. Every caption ends with a call to action — a DM keyword, a link, a question. **No caption goes out without one.**
3. **Stories (45 min).** 3–5 a day using polls, questions, quizzes and sliders. Stories are our conversation surface; the feed is our discovery surface.
4. **Reply (30 min).** Every comment and DM, within the hour where possible. At current volume this is ~1 comment a day — it will grow, and speed matters more than volume.
5. **DM outreach (30 min).** Follow up with everyone who engages twice. Log every course and workshop enquiry.

**Weekly**
- Send Hiral a one-page Friday note: followers gained, what got saved and shared, enquiries logged, what people actually asked about, what flopped.
- Flag the 3 best audience questions of the week so they can become content.

**Later (once we're past ~1,500 followers)**
- Seed and moderate the IFM community group (WhatsApp/Facebook). Starting a group at 344 followers produces a silent group; wait.

- **You do not need to ask for more content to be made.** If you are ever blocked on assets, something has gone wrong.

# ROLE 2 — Fractional Funnel & Launch Strategist

**Type:** Project engagement, 6–8 weeks. Not a retainer, not a hire.
**Start:** September 2026
**Budget:** ₹90,000 – ₹1,80,000 for the full scope, paid 40% upfront / 30% at midpoint / 30% on delivery

## The goal, stated precisely
**Fill the classes.** Paid seats in IFM's own B2C courses, sold to individual mums.

Corporate and school workshops are a separate business with a separate funnel. **This engagement does not touch them.** If a candidate's proposal drifts toward corporate pipeline work, that's a scope failure — say no.

The success measure is one number: **seats sold per cohort.** Not followers, not reach, not engagement.

## The problem we're paying them to solve
IFM has strong content, eleven original financial-literacy games, a founder who teaches well, and an audience that engages above the category median. It has no path that reliably takes a stranger from an Instagram post to a paid seat — and no ask in any caption. We want that path designed, built once, and handed over.

## The arithmetic they have to solve
Small warm audiences typically convert somewhere in the region of 1–2% of followers into paid seats on a well-run launch. Treat that as a planning range, not a promise — but at **344 followers it implies roughly 3–7 seats.**

So a 20-seat cohort cannot be filled from Instagram at current size. The gap has to close from three directions at once, and the strategist must address all three:

1. **Warm first** — past workshop attendees and referrals. Fastest, and probably sufficient for cohort #1 on its own.
2. **Gated games** — convert 344 followers into a list far larger than 344, by giving the games away for contact details.
3. **Audience growth** — Role 1's job, feeding cohorts #2 and #3.

Anyone who proposes filling classes purely from social growth has not done this arithmetic.

## Scope of work — deliverables, not activities

1. **Activate the existing warm list — do this first.** Every past workshop attendee, everyone who has ever enquired. Find them, get them into one place, and design the sequence that offers them a seat. This is the fastest route to a filled cohort and it does not depend on social growth at all. *(See the open question below — we need to establish what we actually hold.)*
2. **Course positioning.** A named course with a fixed price, a fixed next cohort date, a seat count, and a one-line promise a stranger understands in five seconds. Written, not discussed.
3. **The ladder.** Design the free → free → paid sequence. Specifically: turn our existing games into gated lead magnets that capture email/WhatsApp, feeding a free masterclass, feeding the paid course. **Eleven games and not one of thirteen tracked competitors has a single one.** They are currently used as content; they should be the top of the funnel.
4. **List infrastructure.** Email and/or WhatsApp capture and sequences. We are currently entirely dependent on rented social platforms and hold no list of our own. Specify the tools and set them up.
5. **Landing page.** Copy and structure for the course page — written by them, built by us. Must state price, date, and seat count.
6. **Launch calendar.** A dated 4-week launch plan for cohort #1, specific enough that Role 1 can execute it without further strategy input.
7. **Handover.** A written playbook plus a live session so Hiral and Role 1 can run launch #2 without them.

## Open question to settle this week
**Do we hold contact details for past workshop attendees, and how many?**

There is no attendee, registration, or subscriber list anywhere in the project repo. It may exist in Google Drive, a Sheet, or Hiral's inbox — but it needs to be located and counted before the strategist starts, because deliverable #1 depends on it and it is very likely the difference between filling cohort #1 and not.

If no such list exists, that is itself the finding, and capturing contact details at every future workshop becomes an immediate standing rule.

## What we provide
- Brand Guidelines doc, full content archive, competitor tracking data, follower history, all eleven games, whatever attendee records exist, and access to Hiral.

## How we assess them
Ask every candidate for:
- **Two prior launches with actual numbers** — list size, conversion rate, seats or revenue. If they answer with "engagement grew" or "we built brand awareness", end the conversation.
- **One launch that failed, and why.** Anyone with real launch experience has one.
- Evidence they've worked with **course/cohort/education businesses in India specifically.** Indian price sensitivity and WhatsApp behaviour do not resemble the US playbook.
- **A straight answer to: "we have 344 followers and want to fill a cohort — where do the seats come from?"** The right answer starts with the warm list, not with growth.

## Red flags
- Wants a long monthly retainer instead of a scoped project.
- Proposes "more content" or "a content strategy" as the fix. We have 38 finished pieces unpublished.
- Proposes growing the audience first and launching later. That's a 12-month answer to a now problem.
- Drifts toward corporate/B2B pipeline work. Out of scope, separate business.
- No experience with email or WhatsApp — they'll build another social-only funnel and we'll be back here.
- Won't commit to written deliverables with dates.

## Where to find them
- Indian creator-economy and cohort-course circles — look at who ran launches for Indian educators and finance creators you respect, and ask them directly.
- Referrals from other founders selling courses in the ₹5k–₹25k range. Ask in founder communities rather than searching marketplaces.
- Growth/marketing communities rather than agencies.

# Platform priorities for this goal

Since the goal is **filling classes with individual mums**, not corporate bookings, the three platforms rank very differently from how they're currently treated:

| Platform | Priority | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Instagram** | **1st — nearly all effort** | Where mums are, where the games demo well, where the reels already outperform static 5×. This is the discovery engine. |
| **Facebook Groups** | **2nd — underused** | Existing mum communities are the single best place to reach this exact audience. Not feed posting — *participating in other people's groups*, then eventually running our own. Should be part of Role 1's outbound hour. |
| **LinkedIn** | **3rd — low effort, don't drop it** | Serves the *corporate* funnel, which is a separate business. Keep Hiral posting 2–3×/week from her personal profile because it feeds that other line, but it does **not** fill classes and should not consume Role 1's time. |

**LinkedIn — revised 17 Aug:** Hiral remains the author and the face; Sakshi and Ketaki handle scheduling, formatting and comment replies. Company pages get almost no organic reach; founder profiles do. Don't outsource it and don't let it expand — for this goal it's maintenance, not growth.

**Facebook feed posting is dead weight.** Repurpose into it if it's free, but no original effort. The value on Facebook is entirely in Groups.
