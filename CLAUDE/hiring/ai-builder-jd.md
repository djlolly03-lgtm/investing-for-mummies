# AI Builder

*We teach people money by making them play, not sit through slides. Help us build the things that do it.*

**Company:** Investing for Mummies (plus our corporate and call-centre training builds) · **Location:** Nariman Point, Mumbai — in office
**Reports to:** Aditya · **Level:** junior / first or second job · **Type:** full time

---

## Read this part first

This is not a developer role, and screening it like one will send us the wrong people.

We build a lot: around fifty browser-based learning games, a website, internal dashboards, video
pipelines, client documents. Almost all of it is built **with AI** — we describe what we want, AI
writes it, and then the real work starts: checking whether what came back actually works, finding
the bit that's subtly wrong, and saying precisely enough what's wrong that the next attempt is right.

**That checking-and-describing loop is the job.** It needs curiosity, stubbornness and taste. It does
not need a computer science degree, and we would rather you didn't have one than have one and no
interest in this.

We are happy to teach. We expect to spend real time teaching. What we cannot teach is wanting to
know how things work.

---

## What you'd actually do

**Build things with AI, under supervision at first.** A game screen, a calculator, a landing page, a
one-page report. You'll be shown the tools and the house standards, then given real work on day one.

**Test everything, properly.** Open the game on an actual phone. Play it to the end. Click the thing
nobody clicks. We recently audited our games and found over five hundred things to fix — someone has
to work through lists like that, and notice the ones that matter.

**Run the pipelines we've already built.** We have written recipes for turning session footage into
Instagram reels, slides into videos, data into dashboards. You run them, spot where they break, and
tell us.

**Keep the content catalogue honest.** Thumbnails, descriptions, links that actually open. We once
published a photo as a founder portrait because its description confidently said it was — and it
wasn't. Catching that kind of thing is valuable and rare.

**Write things down.** When you work out how something is done, the next person shouldn't have to
work it out again.

## What we're looking for

| | |
|---|---|
| **Genuinely interested in AI** | You already use it for your own things, unprompted. Not a course certificate — curiosity. |
| **Notices when something is slightly off** | A misaligned button, a number that can't be right, a sentence that doesn't match the picture. This is the core skill. |
| **Says what's wrong precisely** | "It's broken" is no use. "The Next button does nothing on round 3 if you skip the first question" is gold. |
| **Doesn't give up at the first failure** | Most of this work is third and fourth attempts. |
| **Writes clear plain English** | Half of working with AI is describing things well. |
| **Comfortable on a computer** | Files, folders, spreadsheets, browsers. Enough to not be slowed down by the machine itself. |

### Not required — please don't filter on these
- A computer science or engineering degree
- Any programming experience
- Prior work experience in tech
- Specific tools or software

### Good signals
- Has built *something* — a game, a site, a spreadsheet model, an edited video, a Discord bot, a
  working ChatGPT workflow for something boring in their life. Scrappy counts.
- Can explain something they got wrong and what they changed.
- Plays games, or makes videos, or takes things apart.

### Signals to be wary of
- "Prompt engineering expert" on the CV with nothing built to show.
- A list of AI tool names and no example of using one for anything real.
- Wants to do strategy, not hands-on work.

## The filter task — please use this instead of CV screening

CVs will not tell us who is right for this. Ask every candidate to do this before an interview:

**Use any free AI tool to make something small and real** — a web page, a little game, a
calculator, a quiz. Send us:

1. a link, screenshot or screen recording of it,
2. roughly what you asked the AI to do, and
3. **one thing it got wrong, and how you fixed it.**

It does not need to be good. **Point 3 is the part we actually read.**

Anyone who sends a thoughtful answer to part 3 should get an interview, whatever their background.
Anyone who sends nothing, or sends a polished thing with no account of what went wrong, probably
isn't right for this.
