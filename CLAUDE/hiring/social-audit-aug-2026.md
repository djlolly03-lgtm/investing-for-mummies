## The course problem is real, and it's absolute

Across all 12 captured captions:

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Mention a course or workshop | 3 / 12 (25%) |
| **Contain any call to action** | **0 / 12 (0%)** |
| Name a price | **0 / 12** |
| Name a date | **0 / 12** |
| Ask the reader to reply | 2 / 12 |

Not one caption contains "link in bio", a DM prompt, a registration link, a price, or a date.

And all three workshop mentions are **retrospective** — "at our workshop, we discussed…", "they walked in with doubts and walked out with confidence." They are recaps of something that already happened. A reader who wants in has nowhere to go.

This isn't "we don't push enough." **There is no ask at all.** The conversion rate of a post with no CTA is not low, it's undefined.

Both posts that did ask for comments ("Drop your question below 👇", "Tell us in the comments 👇") received **zero comments**. At this account size, asking doesn't work on its own — the audience has to already be in the habit of talking to you.

## The sharper cut: it isn't reels that win — it's faces

The reel-vs-static split is real but too coarse. The 12 posts sort into three tiers:

| Content type | Engagement | Comments |
|---|---|---|
| **Real people** — workshop women, founder story, founder mission | ♥56+10💬 · ♥58+4💬 · ♥42+2💬 — the top three | **16 of the account's 17 lifetime comments** |
| Concept explainers — RBI, repo rate, gold, asset buckets | ♥21–41 | ~zero |
| Graphic carousels — no people | ♥7 | zero |

**Nearly every comment this account has ever received landed on a post with a face in it.**

### The honest wrinkle — teens content was tried once, and flopped

The 5 August "Young Adults Workshop" reel got **♥11 — the worst reel on the account.** Its cover is a dark shot of the backs of heads under a text overlay reading *"Here's what to expect from our Young Adults Workshop."* No faces, no moment, and framed as a promo.

So the lesson is not "workshop content works." It is: **faces + outcome-story beats faceless + promo, on the exact same subject.**

### What follows — the certificate photo as a repeatable format

Certificate-day photos are the precise inverse of the 5 August post: teens grinning at camera, holding proof of completion.

1. **Faces stop the scroll** — mechanically and humanly.
2. **Outcome proof, not a promise.** A promo says "come". A certificate photo says "this finishes, and people leave with something" — the strongest trust signal an education brand has.
3. **The buyer is in the audience, and it isn't the teen.** Followers are mums. A mum seeing teenagers hold investing certificates thinks *"my kid should have this."* Parent aspiration for the child is the most reliable purchase trigger in Indian ed-tech — and it compounds the white-space position, since nobody in the tracked set shows mums what their kids could achieve.
4. **Congratulations posts farm comments** — tag-a-parent, "so proud", name-the-batch. Comments are exactly what this account is starved of.

**The play, split by lane:** feed post to the agency with a story-framed caption (*"8 teenagers now understand SIPs before their first paycheck"*, not *"our workshop happened"*); Story sequence to Sakshi the same day — group photo → one teaching moment → poll (*"Would your teen pass a money quiz?"*) → **DM us TEENS**, landing in her enquiry log. The hub's Certificates tab already tracks posted-Y/N — add the teen batch rows and the motion becomes tracked rather than remembered.

> **⚠️ Consent first, before anything goes live.** These are minors holding certificates with their full names printed on them. Publishing children's faces and names needs verifiable parental consent — India's DPDP Act expects it, and for a brand selling trust to parents one annoyed screenshot is expensive. The fix is cheap: these workshops run on pre-registration, so parents are a one-line WhatsApp away. Whatever the consent status, ensure certificate names aren't legible at posting resolution.

## What the competitor data says about position

From the 3 August synthesis of 13 tracked accounts:

- **Zero of 13 have an interactive game.** IFM has eleven. This is a structural differentiator, and currently it is being used as *content* rather than as a *reason to hand over an email address*.

## What this changes

**1. Re-scope the first hire from community to growth.**
The job at 344 followers is distribution: publish the backlog, run daily Stories, and do outbound commenting on other accounts. Replying to comments is a 20-minute-a-day task, not a role. Revisit a true community hire at roughly 3,000 followers.

**2. Put a CTA on every post, starting with the next one.**
This is free and it is the single biggest gap in the audit. It doesn't need a hire, a strategist, or a funnel. It needs one line at the end of a caption.

**3. Publish the 38 ready assets before commissioning anything new.**
There is no case for producing more content while a third of a year's output sits finished and unposted.

**4. Make more of what works.** Founder-to-camera and real workshop footage outperform polished explainers on the only metric that indicates conversation. There are 188 raw session photos and videos to cut from.

**5. The goal is filling B2C classes — settled 17 Aug.**
Corporate and school workshops are a separate business with a separate funnel and are out of scope here. Success is **seats sold per cohort**, not followers.

This has a hard consequence. At a typical 1–2% follower-to-seat conversion for small warm audiences — a planning range, not a promise — **344 followers implies roughly 3–7 seats.** A 20-seat cohort cannot be filled from Instagram at current size, however good the funnel is.

So cohort #1 has to be filled from **warm contacts**: past workshop attendees and referrals. Which raises the question below.

### The cohort maths this makes possible

**Segment the warm list before selling to it.** The 72 IFM alumni have already taken the mums' class — selling them the same thing won't work. They're the audience for a *next-level* course, not the beginner one.

**For a BEGINNER mums cohort** *(planning ranges, not promises)*:

|---|---|---|
| **Parent households of IFT + IYA students** — mums who trust IFM but haven't taken the class themselves | 44 | 4–9 |
| **Pipeline prospects** — already asked, never converted | 27 | 5–8 |

**For an ADVANCED / next-level cohort:** 72 alumni → **11–18 seats**.

### What this changes

**A 20-seat cohort is fillable from the warm list without Instagram.** At the ₹14,754 mums average that's roughly **₹2.95L per cohort.**

> *Superseded 18 Aug: current list price is ₹19–21k, batches run 15–18 seats each (18–20 once the new office room is built), and capacity is 36–40 seats/cycle worth ₹6.8L+. The warm-list logic stands; the numbers above are the historical baseline.*

Cohort #1 does not need social, the content agency, or the hub rebuild. It needs an offer, a date, and someone working the existing 143 warm contacts. **That's a this-month job, and it's independent of everything else in this file.**

Which reframes what the social build is for: **Sakshi's eight weeks are building cohorts #3, #4 and #5** — the repeatable machine — not the next launch. Both should run, but they're on different clocks and shouldn't be sequenced against each other.

### Two things worth chasing

- **The 27 pipeline prospects are the most urgent revenue in the business.** They already raised their hands. Nothing in the funnel is warmer, and nobody is working them.
- **Only 17 feedback responses across 116 students (15%).** Testimonials from mums are the single most persuasive asset for selling to other mums, and 99 of them haven't been asked. Cheap to fix, and it directly serves cohort filling.

**Parent-pool caveat:** the 4–9 seats assume the mothers of IFT/IYA students are contactable and distinct from the 72 IFM alumni. Some overlap is likely — a mum who took the class and enrolled her teen. Worth de-duplicating in the dashboard before planning against that number.
