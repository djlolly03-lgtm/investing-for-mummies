## Filming — mandatory, every single class

**No exceptions. Every class gets filmed.**

Why it matters more than it sounds — from the August audit:

| Post | Comments |
|---|---|
| Workshop photos — Streedhan session | **10** |
| Founder story | **4** |
| RBI Monetary Policy explainer | **0** |
| Repo rate explainer | **0** |
| Gold / central banks explainer | **0** |

Real people in real rooms beat polished explainer content, decisively. The most effortful content performs worst. **Don't aim for polished — aim for real.** Phone footage of a mum laughing at Stock Rush beats a beautifully produced explainer, and costs nothing.

### The filming loop

1. **Film** — phone, every class. Wide shots of the room, close-ups of reactions, people playing the games, the moment something clicks.
2. **Upload same day** to the Drive Content Library folder.
3. **Catalogue it in the hub** — one row in the catalogue sheet: title, type (`Session Video` / `Session Photo`), venue, date, a one-line description, status `Raw`.
4. **Cut the best moments into Stories** within 48 hours, while it's current.

**Stories reach existing followers; reels reach strangers.** Class footage currently stops at Stories, so our strongest proof never reaches anyone new — she now flags the best clips for the agency's feed calendar too. *(Raised 18 Aug.)*

**The content supply loop, agreed 18 Aug:** Sakshi uploads raw footage weekly → Aditya produces 3–4 finished videos from the latest material → Sakshi places them. **She should never be blocked for something to post.**

The agency draws from this library for the feed. **She is the supply line for the best-performing content category IFM has** — the hub currently holds 188 raw session assets, and they should be growing every week.

## The five non-negotiables

1. **Film every class, upload and catalogue the same day.**
2. **The outbound block is defended like a meeting.** If it's being skipped, that gets raised the same week — not discovered at review.
3. **Outbound is logged.** 15 comments a day, one tap each in the hub. Uncounted work doesn't happen.
4. **Every enquiry gets logged — with a follow-up date, including far-future ones.** *"My daughter starts in six months"* is a qualified lead that currently evaporates. Dated, it survives until the sales hire can work it. **This is where most of the funnel has been leaking.** *(Raised 18 Aug.)*
5. **The first-hour ritual on every new IFM post.** ~20 women in the office; within an hour of a post going live she walks the floor and gets 6–8 real comments going (rotating who, their words, their consent), then replies to each from IFM. The first hour decides a post's reach, and this is an advantage most accounts don't have. *(Added 18 Aug.)*

# How Hiral tracks this

**The principle: she logs, the hub aggregates, you read.** You should never be chasing her for an update — if you have to ask what the outbound number is, the system has failed.

## Two instruments, and they do different jobs

| | What it tells you | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **The Friday note** | *Why* — what worked, what people asked, what flopped | Weekly, from her |
| **The hub summary** | *Whether* — did the work actually happen | Glance, weekly |

**The note is the primary instrument.** The dashboard verifies it; it doesn't replace the conversation. A number tells you outbound dropped; only she can tell you it dropped because three classes ran that week.

## The only three numbers that matter week to week

Everything else is downstream of these. If these three are healthy, don't go looking for problems.

| Metric | Healthy | Worry | Why it's the one to watch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Outbound comments/week** | 70+ | under 50 | **The first thing to slip, and the only thing that grows the account.** It slips silently — nobody notices on the day. |
| **Classes filmed ÷ classes held** | 100% | anything less | Binary and unambiguous. A missed class can't be recovered. |
| **Enquiries logged/month** | rising | flat or zero | The only metric connected to revenue. |

Follower growth is the *outcome* — check it monthly, not weekly. It's too noisy at this size to read week to week, and reacting to a flat week will push her toward vanity tactics.

## ⚠️ Don't check the outbound log daily

If she sees the log being checked every day, she will optimise for the number rather than the quality of the comments — and fifteen thoughtless comments are worth less than eight good ones.

**Look weekly.** The log exists to catch a sustained slide, not a slow Tuesday.

## Before the hub is ready

The hub rebuild is mid-build. **Until it lands, tracking is simply the Google Sheets plus the Friday note** — which is genuinely sufficient. Don't wait for tooling to start the habit; the sheets hold the same data the hub will later read.

## At the 19 October review

Compare against the August baselines in the targets table above. The logs are the evidence — which is exactly why they have to be filled in as she goes, not reconstructed on a Friday.

# Leveraging the Content Hub

The hub already does about **70% of this job's admin**, and most of it is unused. **She should not be building spreadsheets that already exist as tabs.**

## What's already built and should be used as-is

### 1. Competitors tab → her daily outbound queue
This is the biggest unlock in the hub.

Thirteen tracked accounts, each card carrying **the latest six posts with thumbnails, like counts, and direct Instagram links** — plus positioning, what's working, content pillars, hook style, cadence, and an "IFM action" note.

**That is a pre-built outbound queue.** She opens the tab, clicks an account, and clicks straight through to real, recent posts worth commenting on. No list-building, no hunting. And because the competitor scrape refreshes weekly, **the queue refreshes itself.**

Thirteen accounts × six posts = ~78 live targets, regenerating every week.

> ⚠️ **The roster is too small and must be expanded.** 78 targets against 75 comments a week means she exhausts it and starts repeating. **At least 20 more accounts have already been identified — add them all**, taking the roster to 30+. Favour mid-size accounts (5k–50k followers) where a comment is actually visible, not the big creators. *(Raised 17 Aug.)*

### 2. Published tab → her scoreboard, already built
It already computes engagement rate against the live follower count and tiers every post **🔥 Worked / OK / Low reach**, sortable by likes, comments, engagement or ER%.

**She doesn't build a performance report — she reads this tab.** The Friday note is largely a screenshot plus three sentences.

### 3. Library tab → where her footage lands
`Session Video` and `Session Photo` are existing categories (63 and 125 items, all status `Raw`). The catalogue sheet already carries **status, published date, IG link, reach, likes, comments, saves, shares, notes, venue** as human-editable columns.

The pipeline she needs already exists. She's filling it, not building it.

### 4. Creator Tracker tab → the agency's board
Now that an agency owns the feed, this is the natural home for their calendar and deliverables. **Sakshi reads it so her Stories complement the feed rather than duplicating it** — if the agency posts a compounding carousel on Tuesday, her Tuesday Stories should extend that, not repeat it.

### The three additions, and why they matter

The hub's pattern is consistent: a **Google Sheet** (human-owned) + **a tab in `index.html`** that reads it. Her deliverables fit that pattern exactly.

### A. Stories tracking — the biggest gap
**The hub tracks feed posts but not Stories at all** — and Stories are now her primary surface. Without this, the largest part of her day is invisible at review.

One row a day: date, stories posted, poll/question responses, replies received, link taps, sticker taps. Small, but it's the difference between "she posted a lot" and a number.

### B. Outbound tab — and this is the elegant one
Rather than a standalone log, **roll her outbound counts back into the Competitor cards.** Each card would then show:

> *"34 comments left · 6 replies earned · 3 follows gained"*

That turns the competitor roster from a static intel board into a **performance-ranked outbound queue** — it tells you which of the thirteen accounts actually returns followers, so she can spend her 75 minutes where they convert instead of spreading evenly. Nothing else in the stack answers that question.

### C. Enquiry tab
Every course enquiry, its source, and its status. **This is the sheet that fills cohorts** — the one place where "we posted a lot" turns into "eleven people asked about the September batch."

## Where the work gets logged

**Her logging must not wait for the hub to be finished.** She has been working since July without a measurement baseline — that is the actual gap, and every further week without one is a week that can't be reviewed.
