The work

What you'd actually do

1
Turn an idea into something people can play

Someone needs to explain compounding, or a scam, or a company's own training module. You build the thing that teaches it — a game round, a calculator, an interactive page. Supervised at first, then not. Real work on day one, not a training month.

2
Check that it actually teaches

Open it on a real phone and play it to the end as a first-timer would. Not just "does the button work" — does the idea land? Our last audit found over five hundred things to fix, and the ones that matter are the ones that break understanding.

3
Run the pipelines we've already built

We have written recipes for turning session footage into Instagram reels, slides into videos, data into dashboards. You run them, spot where they break, and tell us.

4
Keep the catalogue honest

Thumbnails, descriptions, links that actually open. We once published a photo as a founder portrait because its description said it was — and it wasn't. Catching that is rare and valuable.

5
Write things down

When you work out how something is done, the next person shouldn't have to work it out again.

For the recruiter

Use this instead of CV screening

CVs will not tell us who is right for this — "AI" is on every CV now and means almost nothing. Please send this task to every candidate before any interview.

The filter task

Use any free AI tool to make something small and real — a web page, a little game, a calculator, a quiz. Then send us:

  1. A link, screenshot or screen recording of it
  2. Roughly what you asked the AI to do
  3. One thing it got wrong, and how you fixed it

It does not need to be good.

Point 3 is the part we actually read. Answer it thoughtfully and you get an interview — whatever your background.
