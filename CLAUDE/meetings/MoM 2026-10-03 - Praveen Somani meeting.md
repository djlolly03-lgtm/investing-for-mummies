## 4. The end-of-course questionnaire (the main thing agreed)
- At the end of the course, give a short **"which door do you need?" form** of 4 to 8 tick-box questions. Its job is to send each woman to the right expert.
- **The categories need soft wording.** Avoid labels like "financial assets", which make people close off. Use friendly categories they can tick, such as family office, trust, will, tax, insurance and selling property.
- A partner really only needs two facts:
  1. **Roughly how much money is involved** (for example "about ₹5 crore"), plus job or income profile.
  2. **What her situation is:** liquid wealth, or an asset that will need to be sold (which means planning the tax and the sale).
- The aim, in Praveen's words: *"get that lady to your doorstep. Let her ring the doorbell."* The form is offered as a free service to find the right expert, not as a sales pitch.
- **Everyone agreed on the call to action: course fee + a free consult.**
- The model Hiral is copying is an ET / 1% Club insurance webinar that ended with a free policy review. The review was done by **screen share, not upload**, so the client never hands over documents. That made it feel low-pressure. Copy that format.

## 5. A warning about selling assets
- People get caught by accepting **token money**. Once you accept consideration, you're bound by the contract, often with no exit clause, while the buyer stays uncommitted.
- (Praveen's dad takes only a silver coin as *shagun*, kept outside the agreement.)
- This kind of advice is part of what the partner network gives clients.

## 6. Delivery format and scaling
- Online-only classes don't hold attention. One-way webinars with no video and no interaction lose people. **Hybrid works best**, but it's hard to do across Surat, Delhi and other cities.
- Millennium Maths came up as a cautionary example: several women said two years of it taught them nothing.

- Praveen is happy to do this. A committee was once formed for a similar programme, but it stalled.
