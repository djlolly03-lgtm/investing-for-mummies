We're all logged into the IFM Instagram, so a DM notification is consumed by whoever opens it first —

a reply at 11pm beats a reply at 10am.

### Send her to a call centre

**Wendy spends one or two days at the Thane call centre.** Aditya to speak to **Roshni (HR)** directly
— not via Arvind, no connection needed. She takes her support phone and works from there.

The point isn't the software. It's seeing how dispositions are captured, how calls get logged, how a
report comes out the other end, and — most usefully — **sitting next to people doing sales calls all
day and hearing what a rebuttal sounds like.**

### What she starts doing now

**Log every call manually.** 15–20 dials a day, 3–4 real conversations. That's a few lines, not a
system. *"These are the 20 people I called, these are the 4 I reached."* Without it, if sales don't
happen we can't tell whether the approach is wrong or the effort isn't there.

**Multi-tag every lead.** Location first — she's already tagging by batch month, which is a start. Then
the reason behind every "no", from a drop-down with free-text remarks beside it.

**Ask diagnostic questions on every no.** Not *"are you working?"* and stop. Is it the location? The
time of day? The time commitment? Would a condensed 5-day suit better than 8 sessions across a month?
Mon/Wed or Tue/Thu? Online or in person?

> **Hiral has been wanting to run a batch for working women and doesn't know how to schedule it.**
> Wendy's conversations are the only place that answer exists.

**Aditya's framing of why this matters most:**

> Sakshi is top of funnel and Instagram gives her data. Asba has everyone who has paid. Hiral talks to
> them anyway. **Wendy is the only person in the business talking to people who do not enter the class.**
> That middle layer is where the intelligence has to come from.

And the manner matters: friendly, not salesy. *"We've been really wanting to do one for working women"*
opens people up. A bare question doesn't.

### Finding leads beyond references

Today Wendy is working almost entirely from references. The push was to think wider, and several
angles came out of the room:

- **Pregnant women** — the point in a woman's life when she has standing at home and is thinking
  forward about her child's future. Reach them where they already go every month: **doctors and
  gynaecologists.**
- **Clubs and associations** — CCI, Bombay Gymkhana, Rotary, **FLO** (present in every city), the
  **IMC Ladies' Wing**, kitty and mahjong groups. Daytime availability is the common thread.
- **Salons** — someone paying ₹2,000 for a blow-dry can pay ₹21,000 for sixteen hours.
- **Coworking spaces** — a free two-hour session as lead generation.
- **Seminars.**

**Sell value, not hours.** Not *"this is what it costs per hour"* — what they save on their own
investments, multiple times over.

**Her job is not to close.** It's to get the person into a room with Hiral for ten minutes. *"That door
has to be opened by you. Hiral makes the sale."* Asba's corporate wishlist is ammunition she already
has; she needs IFM visiting cards.

### Scope

**Sales only. Batch planning stays with Asba.** Wendy can suggest — *"I think we're ready for a batch
in Bandra"* — and then Hiral and Asba decide. *"If there are 22 seats for January, she fills 22 seats.
That's it."*

**Where it landed:** Hiral proceeds her way for three months. **Both positions are on the record so we
can look back in February and see which was right.**

### What was agreed regardless

*"Make a rulebook and stick to it."* This removes the thing that actually hurt before — running a batch
for three people out of a sense of commitment.
