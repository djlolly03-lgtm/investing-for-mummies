Sakshi noticed the missing bio link the same day it was raised in the deck. **Her refinement is better than what was in the presentation:**

> **The link shouldn't be the website. It should go permanently to the batches page.**

**Why:** a generic homepage makes the visitor hunt. The `/ifm` registration page holds every batch, updates itself as dates roll, and stays valid every month — so the link never needs changing.

## 3. IFM's structural advantage — the reverse funnel

The sharpest strategic point of the meeting.

| | Path |
|---|---|
| **Creators** *(Rachana, Neha, LXME)* | Built an audience on social **first** → then created courses to sell to it |
| **IFM** | Has **116 students, 50+ paid women, running batches** — and is building the audience **second** |

**We are not behind. We are doing it in the opposite order, and our order is the safer one.** They had to invent a product for an audience; we already have a proven product and are now finding the audience for it.

**Implication:** IFM's content should lead with *proof* — real rooms, real students, real outcomes — because that's the asset the creators had to fake until they had it. That is exactly what the engagement data already says wins.

## 5. Stories reach followers. Reels reach strangers.

A structural point that changes where class footage goes:

- **Everything short-form is currently going to Stories only**
- Stories reach people who **already follow us** — that is retention, not growth
- Reels reach people who **don't** — that is the only discovery surface

> *"The reel can reach random people even if they're not our followers."*

**Action:** class footage goes to **both**. Sakshi cuts for Stories *and* flags the strongest clips for the agency's feed calendar. Filming once, publishing twice.

## 7. The lost funnel — the most expensive gap identified

> *"Your funnel is coming, but all of the funnel is getting lost."*

Today: someone DMs → Sakshi replies → **nothing is recorded.** Thirty days later nobody knows what happened.

**The case that makes it concrete:** *"It's for my daughter, she's coming in six months — message me in June 2027."* That is a real, qualified, future-dated lead. **Right now it evaporates.**

**What's needed:** every conversation logged with a **follow-up date, including far-future ones**, and an outcome. So that when the sales hire lands, they inherit a list: *"these 20 people are ready to be spoken to."*

> **Note: the Enquiries tab already has a `Follow up on` date column** — so the mechanism exists. What was missing is the habit and the instruction to use it for dates months out.

**Action:** Sakshi to verify with sources before this is repeated anywhere.
