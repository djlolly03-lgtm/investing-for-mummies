# Job Description — Enrolment & Growth Executive

**Prepared:** 18 August 2026 · **Lane:** SALES *(see `1 - IFM Role Architecture`)*
**Reports to:** Hiral · **Type:** full-time · **Location:** Mumbai / hybrid

---

## Before writing the ad — one recommendation on the title

The 17 August review called this a **"Sales Head."** I'd argue against that title, for two reasons.

**1. There is no team to head.** A "Head" implies people to manage. This is one person doing the calling themselves. Candidates who want a Head title are usually looking to manage rather than dial, and this role is dialling.

**2. IFM cannot yet fund a Head's salary.** Total revenue to date is ₹15.65L across 116 students. A genuine sales head in Mumbai commands ₹10–15 LPA — that would consume most of a year's revenue.

**What the work actually is:** warm-lead conversion by phone and WhatsApp for an education product. In the Indian market that role is an **Admissions Counsellor / Enrolment Executive**, hires at ₹25–40k/month plus incentive, and there is a deep candidate pool with directly relevant experience.

> **Recommendation: hire an Enrolment & Growth Executive now.** Promote to Sales Head when there are two or more people to lead. Everything below is scoped to that.

## Why this role exists

IFM has three lanes. Two are staffed:

| Lane | Owner | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Broadcast** | Sakshi | Gets people looking |
| **SALES** | **← this role. Currently vacant.** | Interest → payment |
| **Retention & Operations** | Asba | Payment → completion |

Until now, sales has been smeared across people who neither wanted it nor took upside from it. The consequences are visible and measurable:

- **27 prospects are sitting in the pipeline, unworked.** Every one of them raised their hand and nobody followed up.
- **99 of 116 past students have never been asked for feedback** — so there are almost no testimonials, and no referral motion at all.
- **There is no attribution.** Flyers were distributed in person and nobody can say whether a single seat came from it.

**This role exists to close that gap. It is the only role at IFM whose output is measured in rupees.**

## What you don't own

- **Content, Stories, Instagram, LinkedIn** — Sakshi's. She brings people to the door; you convert them.
- **Anything after payment** — Asba's. Batch logistics, scheduling, invoicing, feedback. Once someone pays, you hand over and move on.
- **Curriculum, pricing, teaching** — Hiral's.

> **The two handoffs define the job.** An enquiry arrives from Sakshi the same day it's made. A paid student passes to Asba the moment payment clears. **You live entirely in between.**

## The first 90 days

**Days 1–14 — work the backlog.**
Call all 27 open prospects. Not email — call. Find out why each didn't convert. That single exercise will teach you more about IFM's objections than any briefing, and it should produce the first seats.

**Days 15–45 — mine the alumni.**
116 past students who paid once and enjoyed it. Two motions: **re-enrolment** into a next-level programme, and **referral** — a mum who liked it knows other mums. This is the warmest audience in the business and nobody has ever worked it.

**Days 46–90 — build the repeatable motion.**
Zoho clean and current. A written follow-up cadence. Attribution codes live. Inbound enquiries from Sakshi converting at a rate we can actually measure.

## Who we're looking for

**Must have:**
- **1–3 years converting warm leads by phone** — education, edtech, insurance, real estate, wellness. The product matters less than the motion.
- **Comfortable calling.** Most of this job is phone and WhatsApp. Someone who prefers email will not succeed.
- **Fluent English and Hindi.** Our audience is Indian mums aged roughly 30–50, and the conversation moves between both.
- **Genuinely warm.** These are women who often feel embarrassed about not understanding money. A hard closer will do real damage to the brand.
- **Comfortable with a CRM** and with keeping it honest.

**Nice to have:**
- Education or edtech admissions background — closest analogue
- Has sold to women, or to parents
- Has worked somewhere small enough that they built the process rather than inherited it

**Explicitly not required:**
- A finance background. Hiral teaches; you enrol.
- A degree in anything particular.
- Experience with "high-ticket closing" — at ₹15k this is not that market, and people from it tend to bring the wrong tone.

**The disqualifier:** anyone whose instinct is pressure. IFM's audience is nervous about money. Push them and they don't just decline — they tell other mums.

## How to assess — do a live mock call

**Skip the interview-first approach. This job is a phone call, so test a phone call.**

Give the candidate 20 minutes of prep and this brief:

> *"You're calling Priya. Six weeks ago she DM'd asking about our beginners' course for mums, we sent her the details, and she went quiet. She has two kids, works part-time, and told us she's 'not good with numbers.' The next batch starts in three weeks. It costs ₹15,000. Call her."*

Have someone play Priya, with instructions to be polite, interested, and full of soft objections — *too expensive, no time, need to ask my husband, maybe next batch.*

**What you're grading:**
- Do they ask questions, or do they pitch at her?
- Do they hear "I'm not good with numbers" as the fear it is, and respond to *that*?
- Do they handle "I'll ask my husband" without either dismissing it or collapsing?
- **Do they actually ask for the sale?** Astonishing numbers of candidates never do.
- Would you want your own mother to receive this call?

That last question is the real test. Everything else is technique.

## Open question — does this role also sell to schools and corporates?

**Recommendation: no, not initially.**

IFM has an institutional motion — a schools and colleges pitch deck, and corporate proposals like the ONGC one. It is real revenue and worth pursuing. But:

- **It's a different sale.** Institutional selling is long-cycle, multi-stakeholder, and relationship-led. Converting a warm mum on the phone is short-cycle and emotional. **People who are excellent at one are frequently poor at the other.**
- **It's a different sales cycle length**, which means a different incentive design — per-seat commission doesn't work for a six-month institutional deal.
- **The B2C pipeline alone justifies the hire** and can be measured within 90 days.

**Suggested split:** this role owns B2C seats. Institutional pitching stays with Hiral and Asba for now, and becomes a separate conversation once B2C is running predictably.
