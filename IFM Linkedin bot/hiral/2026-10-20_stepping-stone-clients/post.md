When I sat with a bank relationship manager, he ticked "Regular plan" on my form without a word of explanation.

I didn't know what it meant. I'm not sure he expected me to ask.

Here's what that tick means. A regular plan is bought through a distributor, and their commission is built into the fund's yearly costs. A direct plan is bought straight from the fund house or a direct platform, with no distributor commission, so the yearly cost is lower.

Neither is "wrong". But you should be the one choosing.

And here's the industry pattern I hear about again and again in our batches: the adviser speaks to the husband or the father, not to her. She's treated as the route to the "real" client.

To be fair, many advisers serve women brilliantly. This is about a pattern, not any one firm or person.

How to be taken seriously as the client. Ask:
1. Are you a SEBI-registered investment adviser, or a mutual fund distributor?
2. How are you paid, and by whom?
3. Is this a direct or a regular plan, and why?
4. Can you explain it to me, in my words, before I sign?

Has an adviser ever spoken to your husband or father instead of you?
