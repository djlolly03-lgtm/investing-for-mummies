"Ladies don't need to worry about all this."

Many of us grew up hearing some version of this line. Some of us still hear it: from parents, from in-laws, sometimes from the voice in our own head.

I want to say this kindly, because it's rarely said with bad intent. Usually it's protectiveness, or habit, or "log kya kahenge", or a fear that money talk will turn into a fight.

But here's what I believe: silence about money isn't protection. It's a risk we haven't named yet.

It leaves women out of the decisions that shape their own future, and then unprepared on the day life changes suddenly. Losing my father early taught me that.

What I've seen work for the women in our batches isn't a confrontation. It's a few calm sentences, said more than once:
"I'm not questioning you. I'd like to understand."
"If something happens, I don't want to start from zero."
"Can I sit in on the next meeting with our CA?"

Have you faced this headwind at home? What helped you push past it?

Tomorrow the IFM page is sharing five scripts women in our batches have used to open the door.
