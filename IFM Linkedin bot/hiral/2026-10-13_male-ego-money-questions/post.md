## Post copy (ready to paste)

```text
"Why are you asking? Don't you trust me?"

If you've ever heard this after asking a simple money question at home, this one's for you.

Let's be fair to both sides.

For many men, managing the family's money is how they show care. A question can feel like an audit, as if you're checking their work.

But a question isn't a verdict.
"Help me understand" is very different from "prove it to me."

One woman in our last batch put it perfectly: when someone is already managing the money, "you feel like those are more able hands, so you don't really step in."

That's the habit we're trying to break, gently.

What has helped women in our batches:
Ask about goals before products. "What are we saving for?" lands better than "Why this fund?"
Ask to learn, not to check. "Can you show me how this works?"
Pick a calm moment, not the day the bank statement arrives.

Most husbands, it turns out, are relieved to share the load once it's framed that way.

Has asking a money question at home ever turned awkward, and what helped?
Men, I'd love your view too: how would you want to be asked?
```
