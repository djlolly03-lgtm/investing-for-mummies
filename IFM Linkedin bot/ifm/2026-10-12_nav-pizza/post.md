```text
A fund with a ₹10 NAV is not "cheaper" than one with a ₹500 NAV.

It took a pizza for this to finally click for so many women in our workshops.

NAV is simply the price of one slice: everything the fund owns, minus what it owes, divided by the number of units.
A low NAV means smaller slices. It says nothing about how good the pizza is.

Quick example (illustration only, before costs):
₹10,000 in Fund A at NAV ₹10 = 1,000 units.
₹10,000 in Fund B at NAV ₹500 = 20 units.
Both grow 10%. Both are now worth ₹11,000.

The slice price didn't matter. The growth did.

So look at the pizza instead: what the fund invests in, what it costs each year, how it has done against its benchmark over many years, and whether it fits your goal.

Be honest: did you believe the low-NAV myth?

Educational content, not financial advice.

#Investingformummies #FinancialEducation #FinancialLiteracy #MutualFunds
```
