## 1. The problem it exists to solve

IFM produces content from four directions at once — Hiral's workshops, Sakshi's class
shoots, the Aakara agency, and one-off in-house builds. Before the hub, all four landed
in different Drive folders chosen ad hoc, and the only index was what people remembered.

Three failures in Q3 2026 forced the rebuild. All three are real, not illustrative:

| | What happened |
|---|---|
| **35+ files per class, unfindable** | Sakshi shoots 10–15 videos and ~20 photos every class. Each batch went to a different folder. When a story needed a specific clip, nobody could locate it. |
| **29 finished posts invisible for two months** | Aakara delivered 29 completed posts across July–August. Our sync watched their *planning deck* instead of their delivery folders, so the hub never saw finished work we had already paid for. |
| **Zero useful tags on older shoots** | Early cataloguing used free-text keywords. Searching "Hiral teaching" or "group photo" returned nothing. |

The organising principle behind every fix since:

> **Replace "someone remembers" with "the system does it."**

At 300 assets, memory coped. The catalogue is now 384 and heading for thousands. Memory
does not scale; systems do. That sentence is the whole design rationale.

## 2. What it is, architecturally

A **single self-contained HTML file** served as a static asset. No framework, no build,
no server, no database.

Why it is built this stupidly simple way, on purpose:

- **No build step** means anyone can edit one file and redeploy. There is no toolchain to
  rot, no `node_modules` to reinstall on a new machine.
- **Data lives in Google Sheets**, not in the app. The team edits a spreadsheet they
  already know; the hub re-reads it on load. Correcting a tag needs no developer and no
  deploy.
- **Reads are plain CSV** over `gviz/tq?tqx=out:csv`. No API keys, no OAuth in the browser,
  nothing to expire.

The cost of that simplicity is stated plainly in §8: the password is cosmetic.

### The two-layer data model

```
data.js  (machine-written)  ──┐
                              ├──►  the Library you see
Catalogue sheet (human)     ──┘     sheet wins on any field a human owns
```

`data.js` is regenerated by the daily processor from what is actually in Drive. The
Catalogue sheet carries the 16 fields humans own — status, published date, IG link, reach,
likes, comments, saves, shares, notes, venue, shot, setting, quality, consent, session,
social. Where both have a value, **the sheet wins**.

This split is the single most important design decision in the hub. It means a human
correction is never overwritten by the next automated scan, and an automated scan never
waits on a human. Both sides can run at full speed without a merge conflict.

## 3. The six tabs

| Tab | Question it answers | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 🎯 **On Deck** | "What do I do right now?" | Sakshi |
| 📚 **Library** | "Where is that file?" | Everyone |
| 🎨 **Creator Tracker** | "What has Aakara delivered that we haven't posted?" | Aditya |
| 🏅 **Certificates** | "Which student photos can we publish?" | Sakshi |
| 📊 **Competitors** | "What is everyone else doing?" | Aditya · Hiral |
| 📲 **Published** | "Did it work?" | Aditya · Hiral |

`?who=sakshi` strips it to the daily working view. `?admin=1` reveals delete and
catalogue controls.

### On Deck
Opens on a to-do list, not a dashboard — deliberately, because it is the one tab someone
is expected to use *every day*. Targets: 15 outbound comments, 5 stories. The outbound
queue is rebuilt from the weekly competitor scrape and filtered by two rules that exist
for a reason:

- **Nothing older than 7 days.** A comment on a dead post is wasted effort.
- **Mid-size posts first, not the giants.** On an 80K-like post your comment lands around
  #400 and is never seen.

Tapping ✓ Commented writes to the Outbound sheet and updates the counter immediately.

### Library
The searchable archive, rendered as a **sortable table** rather than a card grid. The card
grid was replaced because finding anything meant clicking "Details" on cards one at a time.
The table shows type, shot, quality, session and status in every row, with one-click filter
chips (Social-ready · Hiral shots · Hero · Testimonials · Certificates · Do not use).

Raw and archived material is hidden by default so the default view stays useful.

### Creator Tracker
Aakara's pipeline: planned → delivered → published. The most valuable thing on it is the
**reconciliation check** — the hub compares every status against what is genuinely live on
Instagram and flags mismatches in both directions. It catches the two mistakes a manual
list always makes: items marked Published that are not on the grid, and items marked
Delivered that quietly went live and nobody updated.

### Certificates
Student completion photos, the strongest social proof IFM has and the most sensitive
content it holds. Names are masked in the view (`Jaya B.`) so the tab is safe to
screen-share. The handle column exists so each student can be @-mentioned when her
certificate is posted — that is what drives the reach.

### Competitors
23 tracked accounts in four kinds: 7 direct rivals, 8 content creators, 7 international
benchmarks, 1 platform. Refreshed automatically every Monday. Anyone can add an account
from the tab itself.

### Published
Live posts with real engagement — the feedback loop for everything else.

## 4. How content gets in

```
1. IFM Content Drop      one shared Drive folder, no naming rules, no subfolders
        ↓
2. Daily processor       07:38 — scans every registered folder, tags, dedupes
        ↓
3. Catalogue sheet       one row per asset, team-editable
        ↓
4. The Library           filter, search, find
        ↓
5. Hiral Media Kit       Hero-quality portraits copy across automatically
```

Only stage 1 involves a person. Existing folders were **not** migrated — the processor
watches those too, so nothing broke when the drop folder was introduced.

### The tag schema

| Question | Values |
|---|---|
| What it is | Photo · Video · Carousel · Reel · Story |
| What's in frame | Hiral portrait / teaching · Group · Students · Testimonial · Certificate moment · Room wide |
| How good | Hero (agency-grade) · Usable · Raw/backup |
| Which class | "Mums batch — 26 Aug" · "Corporate — 4 Jul" |
| Can we publish it | **Social-ready** = strong quality *and* consent verified. **Do-not-use** = restricted; cannot surface in a Social-ready filter. |

Fixed choices rather than free text. That is the entire fix for failure #3 — a
vocabulary you pick from is searchable; a vocabulary you type is not.

Burst shots collapse to a single best frame, the rest retained as backups.

## 5. What runs on its own

| Task | When | Deploys? | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| `daily-content-processor` | 07:38 daily | No — stages only | enabled |
| `ifm-daily-brief-email` | 08:07 daily | No | **DISABLED** since 10 Sep |
| `ifm-followers-daily` | 20:08 daily | No | enabled |
| `weekly-competitor-refresh` | Mon 09:39 | **Yes** — the only one | enabled |

**Only one job may deploy, and it is gated.** `vercel --prod` publishes the *entire
working tree*, not just the files a job touched. So the weekly refresh checks `git status`
first and **refuses to deploy if anything unrelated is dirty**, because otherwise it would
publish another session's in-flight work. See §7 — this guard is currently firing.

Nothing is ever posted to Instagram automatically. Nothing reaches the live hub without
human approval.

## 8. Things that will bite you

**The password is cosmetic.** It is a client-side SHA-256 gate. `data.js`,
`ifm-published.json` and every other data file are **publicly fetchable** by anyone with
the URL. Treat everything in the hub as public. This is an accepted tradeoff, not an
oversight — but it now covers follower counts and, once the Graph token exists, reach.

**Deploy only from inside `CLAUDE/`.** Deploying the parent directory wipes the entire
site and 404s every live URL, including the WordPress iframes. This is the one mistake
that cannot be undone quickly.

**Any deploy publishes the whole working tree.** Not just your files. More than one Claude
session runs on this repo at once. Commit before deploying.

**Sheets must be shared "anyone with the link → Viewer"** or the tab silently shows setup
instructions instead of data. That is what the LinkedIn 401 above is.

**gviz needs `&headers=1`.** Without it, gviz *guesses* how many leading rows are header.
On the catalogue it guessed six, swallowed five rows into the header cell, and returned 339
rows starting at IFM-R06 with a garbled header. It fails silently and plausibly.

**Aakara deliver finished files in folders**, in `Month → Carousels|Reels|Stories → Topic`.
The "IFM Creatives_<Month>" Slides deck is a planning calendar only. Scanning the deck
instead of the folders is what hid 29 posts for two months.

**Reach cannot be scraped** at any login level. Engagement rate is the honest stand-in
until the Graph token is in place. `CLAUDE/content/analytics/fetch_ig.py` is the real
route and is built and tested; it needs a token (see `CLAUDE.md` §Credentials 4).

## 10. Open decisions

1. **Deploy** — 8 days of data waiting, blocked on approval.
2. **Media Kit sharing** — "anyone with link" makes it work for any agency without further
   admin, but the portraits are then reachable by anyone who ever receives that link.
   Named sharing is safer and more work. Your call, not an automation's.
3. **Content Drop** — open to "anyone with link → Editor", or keep it to named people and
   drop the "anybody can post" framing?
4. **Outbound target** — 15/day was set aspirationally and reality is ~2/week. Fix the
   target or fix the process, but not both at once.
5. **Certificates** — who fills in 78 rows of handles and photo links, and by when?
6. **Instagram Graph token** — the pipeline is built and waiting (see `CLAUDE.md`).
7. **Daily brief** — turn it back on, or leave it off deliberately?
